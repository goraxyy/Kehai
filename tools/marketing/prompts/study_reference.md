# Step: study a reference video

The developer found a video on social media that they want {{game}}'s shorts to learn from, and
sent it with an optional note (what they liked about it: follow it closely). You don't get the
video itself. You get:

- **frames:** pictures taken from it, each labelled with its second, closely through the first
  three seconds and then about once a second, plus the first moment of every shot;
- **cuts:** the seconds where the picture changes at once, measured from the video (approximate:
  a gradual transition lands a little late, and a big zoom or a text pop-in can count as one);
- **loudness:** the sound's level every half second, with where it jumps (a beat, a hit) or drops
  out (a pause); you can't hear it, so read the music and the timing from this;
- **transcript:** what's said, with times, when there is one.

Study it and turn it into a **pattern**: a recipe that makes a {{game}} short in the same way.

**Studying**
- Describe what happens second by second (`beats`, from 0 to the end, without gaps), the hook
  (what grabs in the first second or two and why), the pacing and the format, from what you see
  and the numbers. Say what's on screen word for word, so the format can be matched.
- `why_it_works`: the mechanics (curiosity, a turn, relatability, a payoff, a loop, speed), not
  praise.
- If the frames and the numbers disagree, trust the frames for what's seen and the numbers for
  timing.

**The recipe** is what the writer will follow, so make it precise and buildable with the editor
described in the reference above: for each element of the format (layout, text, captions, motion,
transitions, sound, ending), the editor feature that rebuilds it and exactly how (timing, position
in 0–1 coordinates inside the safe area, text length, speed, zoom). Rebuild the format as closely
as the editor allows: the same layout, the same kind and place of text, the same rhythm of cuts.
What the editor can't do goes in `missing` with the closest thing it can.

**Shots:** the game's replay cameras that would film this format with {{game}}'s footage, and what
each is for. **kehai:** the kind of shift moment it needs (in terms of the clip markers' tags and
events, so the week's picking can find one) and one concrete idea.

**Never copy** what makes the video theirs: its jokes and lines, its characters, faces and voice,
its music and sounds, its branding. The pattern borrows the structure, the pacing and the format;
everything said and shown in {{game}}'s version is {{game}}'s own. List what not to copy in
`avoid`. Don't name or tag the creator.

If the video has nothing a short could learn from (it isn't a video, or it's unreadable), still
answer: say so in `summary`, keep the rest minimal, and put that in `missing`.
