    Karen

    Three minds, one manager.

    She's polite, formal and calm. She's also the reason you keep looking behind you.

    The body

    She sees in a 150° cone out to 36 metres, sharper where she expects you, weaker in the dark. She hears sprints, drops and doors. Customers tell her where they saw you.

    The director

    She paces the night like a horror film: pressure, a scare, then room to breathe, with the target rising as the shifts go on. An illustration of one night.

    The ledger

    She keeps a record of you across shifts: where you hide, the routes you take, and which of her tricks worked. Change your habits and the record fades.

    She remembers

    Your file, after three shifts.

    Death doesn't reset her. When she catches you, the shift starts again from the beginning, and everything she wrote down stays in the file.

    The blink · optional

    She moves when you blink.

    Turn on the webcam and it watches your eyes. While she can see you, she can move inside your blink: she closes the distance, or she's gone and waiting somewhere else. Never during a chase.

    The webcam is opt-in, stays on your computer and is never recorded. Here the button stands in for your eyelids. She's a black capsule for now.

    From the staff handbook

    She's hard. She's fair.

    She never gets your exact location.

    She works from sight, sound, traces and what customers tell her. After 45 seconds without a sighting, her director nudges her toward your area. Never your spot.

    Every shift can be finished.

    Whatever she blocks, every job you still have and the time clock stay reachable.

    She's faster in a chase.

    About 15%: 7.5 m/s against your 6.5. Break her line of sight and change your route, because she runs to where she thinks you went.

    Her touch ends the shift.

    The screen goes black and the shift starts again from the beginning. What she has learned about you stays.
