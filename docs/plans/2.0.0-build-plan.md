## 2. Which approach is efficient

The real question: rebuild Karen's decision layer, or keep it and cut what runs on it?

| Approach | What it is | Used by | Cost here | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **A. Keep the architecture, cut the content** | Belief grid, scored actions, HTN recipes, Director and Ledger stay. Fewer actions and tricks, clear names, drafts, combos as data. | Kehai today | Low to medium. Tests and the eval harness keep working. | **Recommended** |
| B. Behaviour tree | Authored decisions in a tree, checked top to bottom | Halo 2, Alien: Isolation | High: the decision layer is rewritten | Good for authored sequences. Learning plugs in less naturally than into a scored choice. |
| C. A state machine with a few modes | Idle → Hunt → Attack → Traps, moved by events | Hello Neighbor (your whiteboard) | Medium | Easiest to read, but it loses gradual scoring and the place learning plugs in |
| D. Goal-oriented action planning (GOAP) | Searches for a chain of actions that reaches a goal | F.E.A.R. | High | Combos come free, but it's harder to keep fair and to debug |

**Why A:**
- **The architecture is what works.** The belief grid and planner (rung C) are the big step: about 6 more detections a shift than the patrols, and the first detection at 31 s against 85–101 s ([KAREN_RESULTS.md](../karen/KAREN_RESULTS.md#what-it-shows)). Learning on top (rungs D, E) made players slightly calmer, not better caught.
- **The clutter is in the content:** 11 goals, 34 tricks and 43 goal–trick links, with 6 goals named the same as a trick ([readchanges.md §1](readchanges.md#1-how-it-works-today-on-one-screen)). Cutting that needs no rewrite.
- **A keeps the results comparable:** the same seeds, bots and metrics measure 2.0.0 against today.
- **The best ideas of B and C come along:** combos ([§7.5](#75-when-several-tricks-pass-the-gate-and-combining-them)) give her a behaviour tree's authored sequences. Hello Neighbor's four modes become the way her actions are grouped and shown ([§6.2](#62-the-actions)).

## 3. Awareness rises slowly

### How it works today

Her eyes don't simply see you or not. They build up a number, **awareness**, from 0 to 1
([`SightSensor.cs`](../../Assets/!_Project/_Game/AI/Scripts/Perception/SightSensor.cs)):

```
while you're visible:   awareness += score × 2.2 × seconds      (:69, the 2.2 at :21)
while you aren't:       awareness −= 0.45 × seconds             (:82, the 0.45 at :23)
score = angle × distance × light × motion × exposure            (:116)
```

| Factor | How it's worked out | Typical values |
|---|---|---|
| angle | 1 at the centre of her 120° view, 0 at its edge | 45° off-centre: 0.44 |
| distance | 1 ÷ (1 + (metres ÷ 8)²) (`:108`) | 5 m: 0.72 · 10 m: 0.39 · 15 m: 0.22 |
| light | 0.35 + 0.65 × the light at your feet | lit aisle ≈ 0.87 · dim ≈ 0.55 · power off ≈ 0.36 |
| motion | 0.3 + 0.7 × how much you move (`:110`) | sprint 1.0 · walk 0.69 · crouch 0.44 · still 0.34 |
| exposure | how many of 3 rays (feet, chest, head) reach you | 1, 0.67 or 0.33 |

At 0.5 she has **seen** you and Pursue can start; at 0.85 it's **confirmed** and Pursue scores highest.

### Worked examples, from the formula

| You are | Score | Seen after | Confirmed after |
|---|---|---|---|
| Walking, 10 m away, dead centre, lit aisle | 0.23 | 1.0 s | 1.7 s |
| Walking, 15 m, centre, lit | 0.13 | 1.7 s | 2.9 s |
| Walking, 10 m, 45° off-centre, lit | 0.10 | 2.2 s | 3.8 s |
| Standing still, 10 m, centre, lit | 0.11 | 2.0 s | 3.4 s |
| Walking, 10 m, centre, dim | 0.15 | 1.6 s | 2.7 s |
| Crouched, a third of you showing, 10 m, 45° off, dim | 0.014 | never: under the 0.02 floor, it counts as unseen and drains | never |

### Why it feels slow

- **Five numbers below 1, multiplied.** Each looks generous; together a typical score is 0.1–0.25, so awareness climbs 0.2–0.5 a second.
- **Any gap drains it.** One moment behind a shelf end or a passing shopper and it drops at 0.45 a second.
- **The meter is only the first delay.** From first sight to her running at you:
  1. awareness reaches 0.5: 1–2 s in a lit aisle (the table);
  2. the decision: at once if her current plan can be interrupted, but Stalk, Ambush, Assist and Withdraw plans ignore a sighting (`KarenBrain.cs:635`);
  3. the chase's warning, a screech, then 1 s before she runs (`TacticsBody.cs`, `ChaseTactic`).

  So walking past her at 10 m in a lit aisle takes about 2–3 s before she moves. The chain, not
  the meter alone, is likely what you're feeling.

### Options

| Option | The change | Effect | Risk |
|---|---|---|---|
| A. More gain | 2.2 → about 3.5 | Everything about 1.6× faster | Faster in the dark and at range too: hiding gets weaker everywhere |
| B. A softer product | For example the average of the factors instead of their product | Ordinary cases speed up most | Harder to explain; also changes the CCTV cameras and possessed shoppers, which use the same eye (`KarenProps.cs:259`) |
| C. Longer reach | Half strength at 12 m instead of 8 m | Mid-range speeds up | Long aisles stop being safe |
| **D. Expectation** | Up to 2× gain where her belief already puts you | She's fast when she's looking in the right place, slow when you surprise her | Little: it uses her belief, never your real position, so fairness rule 1 holds |
| **E. A short grace** | Hold awareness for 0.5 s after losing you, then drain | A shelf end no longer resets her | Little |
| F. A shorter chain | Let her start walking at you during the chase's warning | Saves up to 1 s | The warning has to come before the effect (rule 3). The effect is the sprint, so a walk during it is allowed, but it's your call. |

**Decided: A, D and E** (Q1). I'd recommended D and E first, with A only if still slow; you chose
all three. D fits what she is (an expert who knows where to look) and rewards you for surprising
her, which is pillar 3. Alien: Isolation and Splinter Cell use a building meter for the reason
Karen does: a glimpse through a gap shouldn't start a chase
([Karen.md §3.1](../karen/Karen.md#31-sight--graded-not-binary)).

What the three together do, with A at 3.5:

| You are | Seen after, today | With A | With A, where she expects you (D) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Walking, 10 m, centre, lit | 1.0 s | 0.6 s | 0.3 s |
| Walking, 10 m, 45° off-centre, lit | 2.2 s | 1.4 s | 0.7 s |
| Crouched, a third showing, 10 m, 45° off, dim | never | never: A raises the gain, not the 0.02 floor | never |

- **Tune A last.** D and E go in first. Then A is set as low as still hits the Q1 targets, because
  a high gain weakens hiding everywhere.
- **The deepest hiding stays safe:** the floor is on the score, so a score under 0.02 still counts
  as unseen.

**How it's measured:** a scripted walk-by set, the six cases in the table, timed to "seen" and to
"she starts moving at you". The targets are yours ([Q1](#9-questions-for-you)).

## 5. She stops when she loses sight, and PR #68

### 5.1 Why she stops

1. You break her line of sight. Awareness drains at 0.45 a second (`SightSensor.cs:82`).
2. Below 0.5, Pursue's score falls to 0 ("not seen", `Goals.cs:167–171`).
3. The chase's own running step would carry on: to where she last saw you, led half a second along
   the way you were moving (at most 3 m), giving up after 5 s unseen (`Primitives.cs:350–357`).
4. But after the 2.5 s minimum commitment (`Goals.cs:49`), the next decision picks another goal,
   usually Investigate. The new plan replaces the chase: she stops (`KarenBrain.cs:664`) and sets off
   again at a hurry (4.2 m/s) instead of a run (6.8 m/s).

### 5.2 What PR #68 is

[goraxyy/Kehai#68](https://github.com/goraxyy/Kehai/pull/68), a draft: a **learned motion model** for her belief grid.

- **The problem it solves.** Ten times a second the grid does two things: *correct* (fold in what
  she senses) and *predict* (guess how you moved since). The predict step is hand-written, with 5
  hand-set numbers. #68 learns it from player paths instead.
- **The models it tries**, each one step up:

  | Model | What it is |
  |---|---|
  | M0 | Today's step, kept bit for bit |
  | M1 | Today's step with its 5 numbers fitted to data, plus "players keep away from Karen" |
  | M2 | A log-linear model: each cell's chance of moving on and which way, from a weighted sum of features |
  | M3 | M2 plus a small neural network per cell (48 → 32 → 32 → 9) |
  | M4 | M3, also knowing which way you were heading |

- **How it was built** (`tools/belief/README.md` on its branch):
  1. The predict step was put behind one interface, `IMotionModel`, with today's as M0.
  2. 288 bot shifts were recorded (216 to train on, 72 to check), logging every piece of evidence
     so the filter can be re-run exactly in Python.
  3. **Gate 1:** Python's re-run must match C# on every sample. The worst gap was 0.0000076.
  4. **Training, in PyTorch, in two stages.** Stage 1 rolls the model forward from your true cell
     for up to 30 s with no evidence. Stage 2 runs the whole filter with the model inside and trains
     on how much probability it gave your true cell: "training through the filter", a learnable
     histogram filter (Jonschkowski and Brock, 2016).
  5. **Export:** the weights are written into a generated C# file. **Gate 2:** the C# and PyTorch
     models must agree to 0.00001.
  6. **Test:** 144 held-out shifts, including the cautious player, scored by shadow filters on the
     same evidence; then 48 shifts where she acts on the best model.
- **Where it stands:** paused before training, which takes about 5 hours (step 4 above). 47 files,
  6,546 lines added. Branched before #64–#66. Training waits for your explicit go.
- **The bar it must clear:** beat M1, the tuned hand step, on how much probability she gives your
  true cell while she can't see you. Today rung C scores −7.40 and a random guess −8.46.

### 5.3 The fix: keep hunting on belief

- **This goes in the running step that the chase and Hunt's own move share
  ([§6.2](#62-the-actions)),** so it works whichever of the two is running:
  1. While she sees you: run at you, as the chase does now.
  2. When she loses you: keep the action and keep running, toward where her grid says you'll be a
     couple of seconds from now, not where she last saw you.
  3. At a fork, take the way with more belief behind it.
  4. Hand over to Search when she reaches that spot and you're not there, after about 6 s without
     a fresh clue, or when the Director calls a recovery.
- **Fix the cause:** Hunt's score fades with the time since she last saw you, instead of
  dropping to 0 the moment awareness does.
- **It works with today's predict step.** #68 makes "where you'll be" better; this doesn't wait for it.
- **Still fair:** she follows her belief, never your real position (rule 1), and she's only 5%
  faster than you, so breaking sight and turning a corner still loses her.

This replaces [readchanges.md §2.3](readchanges.md#23-keep-sprinting-after-she-loses-sight).

## 6. Actions and tricks

### 6.1 The words

- **Action** (today "goal"): what she's trying to do right now. Every action is scored twice a second and the best one wins.
- **Trick** (today "tactic"): a concrete way to do an action, with a warning, a cost and a cooldown.
- **Its own move** (new): what an action does with no trick at all. Every live action needs one or a live trick, so drafting tricks can never leave her standing still.
- **Draft:** your short-term list of tricks to improve or remove soon. Each one comes back reworked or is removed. Until then the trick stays in the code and the library, so ids, Ledger records and rung F's numbering don't move, but the gate refuses it with "draft" ([readchanges.md §2.4](readchanges.md#24-draft-tricks)).

## 7. Your questions, answered

### 7.1 Tell

- **What it is:** the warning a trick gives before it acts: a sound, a flicker, a shopper freezing.
- **In the code:** a step in each trick's recipe (`Tell`, `Primitives.cs:216`). It holds at least
  0.8 s before the effect. A test checks every trick declares that much (`KarenTests.cs:86`), and the
  auditor measures the real gap in play (`FairnessGuard.cs:40`).
- **Why:** fairness rule 3, "every trick warns you" ([Karen.md §9](../karen/Karen.md)). It's also your
  pillar "no dumb screamers": a scare with no warning is the cheap jump scare you want to avoid.
- **Example:** Spill: a bucket clanks for 1 s, then she kicks it over.
- **Elsewhere:** the wind-up before a boss swings in Dark Souls, or a lorry's reversing beep.

### 7.2 Withdraw

- **When it's chosen:** when you're more scared than the Director wants, or in a recovery window.
  Its score is 0.05 + 1.2 × (panic − target), plus 0.45 in a recovery (`Goals.cs`, the Withdraw case).
- **What it does:** walks to the indoor region farthest from where she thinks you are, plays hold
  music over the PA as its warning, and idles facing away (`TacticsMovement.cs:157`).
- **Why it matters:** it's the low point of each wave. In a recovery the Director blocks Pursue,
  Herd and Ambush (`KarenDirector.cs:191`) but not Investigate, Sweep, Flush or Deny, so without
  Withdraw she'd keep searching near you. Alien: Isolation does the same when its menace gauge
  peaks: the Director calls the Alien away.
- **"Stalk-and-withdraw" is different:** it's the `stalk` trick: walk to the end of your aisle,
  footsteps, look at you for 2 s, leave without chasing (`TacticsBody.cs`, `StalkTactic`). Your note
  "leave after being seen, or blinked if that's on" changes its ending from a fixed 2 s to "until
  you see her, or you blink".
- **Recommended:** keep Back off live, and draft only the stalk trick ([Q5](#9-questions-for-you)).

### 7.3 The warning step

The guide called layer 2 of the fairness gate "the warning step". It's the `Tell` step in each
recipe (7.1): the step that plays the warning and makes her wait before the effect. Its purpose is
rule 3. Layer 1 (the gate) decides whether a trick may start; layer 2 makes it warn you; layer 3
(the audit) checks what really happened.

### 7.4 The audit

- **What it is:** `FairnessGuard`. It watches what really happens and counts anything against the rules. It never stops anything.
- **What it checks:**
  - each effect came at least 0.8 s after its own trick's latest warning (`CheckEffect`, `FairnessGuard.cs:40`);
  - no tier 2+ trick while you're settling in or recovering (`CheckPacing`).
- **Why both a gate and an audit:** the gate prevents; the audit catches what slips past. That's
  recipe bugs, and a trained policy finding loopholes (rung F with its shield off). Its first runs
  caught real bugs: the chase and mimicry acting before their warning, the favour with none, PA
  lines logged before their chime.
- **What comes out:** "fairness violations" in every ablation (0 in the last one) and a warning in the log.
- **Elsewhere:** a football video referee, who doesn't stop play but reviews what happened.
- **Its gaps today:** six tricks have never run under it; overtime will log a false alarm on shift
  10; nothing checks a warning reaches a sense you still have (rule 3); the Director's hint is only
  tested for its size (`KarenTests.cs:418`), not its conditions.

### 7.5 When several tricks pass the gate, and combining them

**Today, when several pass:**
1. Each goal lists its tricks, and the gate drops the ones that fail: locked, wrong tier, cooling down, too expensive, or their own conditions.
2. Every trick left is scored: by the bandit on rungs D and E, by its written-in guess on C (`HtnPlanner.Plan`, `Goals.cs:250`).
3. She takes the best one that turns into a real plan; the rest are fallbacks, best first.
4. **Only one trick runs at a time.** Passing the gate together doesn't combine them.

**Combos exist only inside tricks:** Blackout is lights out, then wait on the breaker route
(`TacticsFacility.cs:55`); Funnel is crate walls on every way but one, then wait by the open one
(`TacticsBody.cs:219`).

**Is combining an open question? Yes**: no code does it.

**Decided (Q12): a room for combos that she fills herself, through RL.** I had proposed 3–5
hand-written combos; you want her to find and improve them on her own.

**The combo room:**
- **What it is:** a place in her memory for chains of tricks, such as `Blackout → Ambush, within
  20 s`. For each chain it keeps how often she's used it and how well it paid off. It starts empty.
- **How Kehai uses it:**
  - At each decision, the learner sees what she did last: which trick, how long ago, and whether
    its effect is still running (lights still out, a door still blocked).
  - Then it picks the next trick. If Ambush right after Blackout keeps paying off, its value rises
    and she chains them more often.
  - A chain that pays off often is written into the room with its numbers, so the thought log and
    the shift report can say "she found Blackout → Ambush on shift 6".
- **Real-world example:** a phone keyboard that learns which word you type after another and
  starts suggesting the pair. In RL, a learned chain that becomes one bigger move is called an
  *option* (Sutton, Precup and Singh, 1999).

**Which learner, in two steps:**

| Step | Learner | Why then |
|---|---|---|
| 1. With 2.0.0 | Rung E's bandit, plus "her last trick" as context (a contextual bandit) | 4 live tricks make 16 ordered pairs: few enough to learn in hundreds of headless shifts, cheaply, and easy to explain |
| 2. When drafts come back | Rung F's PPO network, with her last trick and its running effects added to what it sees | 10 live tricks make 100 pairs, and chains of three make 1,000: too many for a bandit's counts. The network spreads what it learns across similar situations. |

**Fairness stays in charge. The learner only chooses among what the gate allows:**
- **Each part passes the gate when it starts**, not only the first: tier, cooldown and budget are
  checked again.
- **Each part gives its own warning** (rule 3, unless Q17 changes it).
- **Tension adds up:** a chain costs its parts' tension together, so the Director's budget limits
  how long it can run (rule 4).
- **"Still winnable" (rule 6) checks everything active at once**, not just the new trick, for
  example two blocked doors together. That's a gate change: today each trick checks alone.
- **The audit watches what the learner does,** which is its job: a trained policy is the likeliest
  thing to find a loophole.
- **The reward stays the learner's reward:** pacing panic toward the Director's target, so she
  learns chains that serve the wave, not only kills.

**The honest test:**
- The combo learner must beat the same learner without the "last trick" input, paired by seed,
  with the cautious player held out.
- A planted check: on a test layout where Blackout → Ambush is known to pay off, the learner must
  find it within a set number of shifts.

**Today's welded combos:** Funnel is deleted, and Blackout is split into its two halves (lights
out, then Ambush), so she has the parts to rediscover both. The risk: until she finds the chain,
Blackout is weaker than today. [Q18](#9-questions-for-you) asks whether to keep Blackout whole until the learner beats it.

**Several actions can offer the same trick or chain** (your point 35): Ambush can serve both Lie in
wait and Corner. Today's confusion is shared names, not shared tricks.

**How PyTorch is used in #68.** The whole filter, predict and correct, is written in PyTorch so
that gradients flow back through every step over time. The loss is how little probability the
filter gave your true cell. Training runs on the CPU in under 2 GB, cut into 20 s pieces, and only
the finished numbers go into the game.

**Why PyTorch:**
- **Rung F had no choice:** ML-Agents' trainer is built on PyTorch (`tools/rl/requirements.txt`).
- **#68 needed automatic gradients** through a whole filter run, and PyTorch was already installed
  for RL, so the two share one environment.
- **The game never runs PyTorch:** #68 ships plain C# numbers and rung F an ONNX file.

**Other tools that could fit:**

| JAX | The same job as PyTorch for #68; no gain here |
| Stable-Baselines3 | PPO without ML-Agents, but it would need its own bridge to Unity, which ML-Agents already is |

**Recommended:** with four live tricks, PPO has little to learn about which trick to pick. So the

Rung F's PPO returns as step 2, once drafts come back.

### 7.8 Removing "every trick warns you"

You want to remove this pillar. Here is what it would change.

**What it is today:**
- **The rule:** fairness rule 3. Every trick plays a warning at least 0.8 s before it acts, through
  a sense you still have (`minTellLead`, `KarenConfig.cs:117`; [Karen.md §9](../karen/Karen.md)).
- **In the code:** a `Tell` step in every recipe (7.1), a test that every trick declares one
  (`KarenTests.cs:83`), and the audit measuring the real gap in play (`KarenBrain.cs:731`, `:740`).

**What each live trick does without it:**

| Trick | Today | Without the rule |
|---|---|---|
| The chase | A screech, then 1 s before she sprints | She sprints the moment she decides. At 6.8 m/s against your 6.5, that 1 s was your 6.5 m head start, and a catch ends the shift. |
| Blackout | A ballast whine for 1.2 s, then the lights die | The lights die with no notice |
| Ambush | Silence falls for 1 s, then she waits, silent | She waits with no cue that she's waiting |
| Bin tamper | A lid clatter for 0.9 s, then the bin refills | The bin refills; you find out when you look |
| Ask the customers | Footsteps as she walks over | Unchanged: her footsteps aren't a warning step |

**What else it changes:**
- **Your pillars pull both ways.** "No dumb screamers" (fixes FNAF) needs warnings; a scare with
  none is the jump scare you wanted to avoid. "Efficiency → unexpectedness" is served by removing
  them.
- **Two-way adaptation loses half.** Today you learn her warnings and react to them: the whine
  means run for the breaker. Without them you can learn her habits, but you can't react in the
  moment.
- **The combo learner gets stronger, and harsher.** With no warning rule, RL will learn to strike
  unannounced wherever it pays, because nothing tells it not to. The audit's first runs caught
  exactly that: the chase and mimicry acting before their warning (7.4).
- **Awareness option F becomes moot:** there's no warning to shorten.
- **What has to change with it:**
  - the test and the audit's rule-3 check;
  - the marketing claims (`facts.json` "warnings", MARKETING.md's "warns you before her tricks");
  - the LLM test player's briefing, which tells it every trick is telegraphed (`tools/eval/llm_agent.py:35`);
  - Karen.md, DECISIONS.md §5 and the How Karen Learns guide.
- **The sounds don't have to go.** The whine, the clatter and the screech can stay as atmosphere.
  They just stop being a promise.

**Options:**

| Option | What it is | Fair? | Scary? |
|---|---|---|---|
| a. Remove it entirely | No trick must warn | Least: a catch can come from nowhere | Most surprising at first; repeated deaths with no warning read as cheap |
| **b. Warn only for what can end your shift** | The chase, and any kill move later, keep a warning; Blackout, Bin tamper, Ambush and the traps may act silently | Deaths stay readable | Most tricks become surprises |
| c. Keep it, but shorter and subtler | A minimum of about 0.4 s, and warnings in the world (a flicker, a shopper glancing) rather than sounds | Yes | A bit more than today |
| d. Keep it as it is | — | Yes | As today |

**Recommended: b.** It gives you the unexpectedness where it costs least, and keeps warnings
where a surprise ends your shift. Action games use the same scale: the deadlier an attack, the more
readable its wind-up ([Game Developer, "Enemy attacks and telegraphing"](https://gamedeveloper.com/design/enemy-attacks-and-telegraphing)).
Left 4 Dead gives each special infected its own sound and music cue for the same reason
([bit-tech's guide](https://www.bit-tech.net/reviews/gaming/pc/complete-guide-to-left-4-dead/6/)).
The decision is [Q17](#9-questions-for-you).

## 11. Terms

| Term | What it is | How Kehai uses it | A real-world example |
|---|---|---|---|
| Utility AI | Score every option with a formula and do the best one | Her goals (actions in 2.0.0), scored twice a second | The Sims choosing what a Sim does next |
| HTN (hierarchical task network) | Break a big task into smaller steps until each step can be done | A trick becomes walk → warn → act → leave | A recipe app breaking "make lasagne" into steps |
| Behaviour tree | A tree of decisions checked from the top every tick | Not used; approach B | Halo 2's enemies; Unreal Engine's AI tools |
| State machine | A few modes and the events that switch between them | Not used; Hello Neighbor's four modes | A traffic light |
| GOAP | A planner that searches for a chain of actions reaching a goal | Not used; approach D | F.E.A.R.'s soldiers |
| Learnable histogram filter | A grid filter written so its predict step can be trained end to end | PR #68's stage 2 | Robot localisation research (Jonschkowski and Brock) |
| Contextual bandit | A bandit that also sees the situation before choosing | Proposed next learner for tricks | Choosing which news story to show a reader |
| ONNX | A file format for a trained network that many engines can run | Rung F's policy and the blink CNN | Running one model in both Windows apps and phones |
