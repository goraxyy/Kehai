## 1. How to use this plan

**Tick as you go.** Change `- [ ]` to `- [x]` in the same commit as the work. A task is done when
its *done when* holds and its tests pass.

**Two builders work side by side:**

| Tag | Who | Does |
|---|---|---|
| **[C]** | Claude | Logic and heavy code: her map and grid, prediction, the tricks' logic, the Director, the learner, the bots, tests and the testing runs |
| **[B]** | The second agent (it reads `AGENTS.md`) | Level design and small code: props built from code, things you pick up, highlights, settings, screen effects, drawings on the map, and the claims in the docs |
| **[You]** | The owner | Decisions, playtests, and anything done in the editor |

**Rules for working side by side:**
- **Branches.** `0.2.0` is the PR branch.
  - [C] works on it in `.claude/worktrees/0.2.0`.
  - [B] works on `0.2.0-b`, in its own worktree cut from `0.2.0` (`.claude/worktrees/0.2.0-b`).
  - At each sync point, [C] merges `0.2.0-b` into `0.2.0` and runs the tests; [B] then merges `0.2.0` back into `0.2.0-b`.
  - At the end there is one PR: `0.2.0` → `main`.
- **Never two builders on one file.** Every task lists its files. A file that isn't on your task's list belongs to the other builder: write a request in [§7](#7-requests-between-builders) instead of editing it.
- **Hand-offs go through an API.** Where a feature needs both builders, [C] writes a small API first (named in the task), and [B] builds on it.
- **One Unity at a time:** the owner's editor, or one batch job holding `~/TokenLimit/marketing/state/heavy.lock`. Do the non-Unity work first, then ask.
- **The repo rules hold:**
  - code and docs only;
  - authored data goes in C#, and props are built from code, as `CrateWall.Spawn` does;
  - nothing you run makes sound;
  - commits carry no AI credit.

**About 7.5 m/s.** I've read "the speed" as her sprint; her walk (3.4) and hurry (4.2) stay.
- **Against your sprint:** 7.5 is 1.15× your 6.5, up from 1.05×.
- **What that means:** she gains 1.0 m/s on you. From 12 m away, she catches you in 12 s of straight running, so a corner or a door still has to save you.
- **What follows:** the marketing fact "5% faster in a chase" changes too (task B5.1).

#### About the cone

Three numbers change: the width (150°), the range (36 m) and the half-strength distance (16 m:
at 16 m away her eyes work at half strength). The cameras and possessed shoppers keep their own
eyes, so the 8 m that's written into `SightSensor` today becomes a setting: 16 for Karen, 8 for
them.

**How fast she sees you,** walking in a lit aisle, at today's gain (2.2) and without D:

| You are | Today | With the new cone |
|---|---|---|
| 10 m, dead centre | 1.0 s | 0.5 s |
| 10 m, 45° to her side | 2.2 s | 0.8 s |
| 10 m, 70° to her side | Outside her view | 4.1 s |
| 25 m, dead centre | Out of range | 1.3 s |
| 36 m, dead centre | Out of range | 2.3 s |
| Crouched, a third of you showing, 10 m, 45°, dim | Never | 6.1 s, unless the floor rises |

**What follows:**
- **She beats your targets at today's gain** (0.7 s at 10 m, 1.5 s at 15 m off-centre). So option A, tuning the gain, turns it *down*, to about 1.65, before D makes her faster where she expects you.
- **Hiding needs a higher floor.** The crouched case scores 0.037, above today's 0.02 floor, so she'd see you. A floor of about 0.04 keeps it at "never" and still lets her see you walking or standing anywhere in range.
- **Long aisles stop being safe.** She sees down most of the sales floor in about 1–2 s.
- **Her "looked and found nothing" sweep has to grow.** Today it checks 70 cells a tick, picked from the whole circle of her range (`Sensorium.cs:73`, `:234`), and it uses its own copy of the 8 m. At 36 m the circle holds about 4× the cells, and with 16 m almost all of them are worth checking, so the sweep needs about 4× the budget to check each cell as often as today. Its cost is measured and capped.
- **Her gaze spotlight grows with her range:** it reaches 70% of it, so 25 m, and its angle is capped at 90° (`KarenBody.cs:125`).

## 3. Decision D1: how prediction works

**Why she needs it.** Four things need to know where you're going and when you'll get there:
- **Cut** runs to that spot;
- **Throw** aims at it;
- **Ambush** waits on your way;
- **Hunt** follows you after losing sight.

**Decided (D1, 5 November): guesses about your route, not a second grid.**
- **What a guess is:** one route you might be taking, with a weight. For example: "to the stockroom door, by the end of aisle 4: 55%".
- **How Kehai would use it:**
  - While she hunts, she keeps 3–6 guesses.
  - When the Hunt ends, she drops them.
  - Her belief grid stays the one record of where you are now.
- **Real-world example:** a sat-nav guesses which turn you'll take and re-routes when you miss it. Radar does the same for many aircraft at once, a method called multiple hypothesis tracking ([Reid, 1979](https://doi.org/10.1109/TAC.1979.1102177)).

**How she makes the guesses:**
1. **Where you might be going:**
   - your next task: the one nearest her belief (`KarenBrain.LikelyNextJobRegion`);
   - the exits and the staff room;
   - the breaker box, when the lights are out;
   - your usual routes, from the Ledger.
2. **Two guesses are always there:** you turned back, and you're hiding close by.
3. **Each guess becomes a route** on her region graph, from where she last saw you.
4. **Its weight** comes from three things: how well it fits the way you were heading, how much of her belief lies along it, and how often the Ledger has seen you take it.

**Depth is a time window.** For each spot on a guessed route, she works out when you could get there:
- **earliest:** at your sprint, 6.5 m/s;
- **likely:** at your walk, 4.0 m/s;
- **latest:** crouching, 2.5 m/s.

She looks at most 8 s ahead. Beyond that the guesses spread too wide to race to.

**Implication: what she concludes when you're not there.** She checks a spot, finds no one, and
asks what that means:

| When she checks the spot | What it implies | What she does |
|---|---|---|
| Before your earliest time | You can't be there yet: you're still on the way | Waits, silent, until your window closes |
| Inside your window | The longer you don't come, the less likely this guess is; the others gain | Holds a little, then moves to the next best guess |
| After your latest time | You didn't take this route, or you stopped: you turned back, or you're hiding on it | Drops the guess; searches its route, or follows the turn-back guess |

This is Bayes' rule on the guesses, with "she looked and you weren't there" as the evidence. Her
grid already learns the same way from places she looked and found nothing.

**An example.** She sees you sprinting east down aisle 4, 20 m from the stockroom door, then loses you.
1. **Her guesses:** the stockroom door 55%, turned back to the lobby 20%, hiding in aisle 4 or 5 15%, the cold-storage door 10%.
2. **Your window at the stockroom door:** 3.1 s at the earliest, 5 s walking, 8 s crouching.
3. **She Cuts** down aisle 5 and reaches the door at 2.6 s, before you can. She waits.
4. **At 5 s, nobody has come.** Only a crouching you could still arrive, so the door guess drops from 55% to 20%. "Turned back" is now the strongest guess (36%), so she heads back toward the lobby, checking aisle 5 on the way (27%).

**The alternative: a second grid that looks ahead.**
- **How it works:** copy her grid and run its predict step forward, say 3 s.
- **Why not for Cut:** every predict step spreads belief out. So 3 s ahead it's a blur, with no single spot to race to and no time to beat. It also knows nothing of where you're going.
- **Where it would do:** the next 1–2 s, which is all Hunt on belief needs. The guesses cover that too, so one system does both.

**Later:** PR #68's learned motion model can feed the guesses, because its M4 model knows which way
you were heading.

**D1 is decided:** prediction is built as guesses, with time windows and implication (task C3.1).

## 5. Tests and metrics

**Primary metrics,** fixed before any run, as approved:
1. Seconds from first being visible to her moving at you, over the walk-by set.
2. Metres between her and your real position 5 s after she loses sight, paired by seed.
3. Fairness violations: must stay 0, under rules 1, 2 and 4–8.
4. For the combo learner: setpoint error, against the same learner without "her last trick", paired by seed.

**Secondary:**
- detections, first detection, catches and setpoint error;
- seconds she spends outside her area, which must be 0;
- belief accuracy on the new scale;
- Hunts per shift, and seconds spent sprinting;
- catches where you hadn't seen her in the 5 s before;
- catches within 5 s of a throw;
- how often Cut's spot was right;
- sightings from beyond 18 m, which can't happen today.

**The ablation:**
- rungs C and E, three training players (efficient, skittish, reckless), three careers, shifts 3–8;
- the cautious player is held out: reported on its own, never used to tune;
- negative results are reported as they come.
