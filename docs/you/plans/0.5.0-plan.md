## 7. Item 2: bots into agents

**Your answer (Q9):** better test players, using AI/ML, close to reality, and adaptable. So the
bots stay the trained kind and get better at both; a language-model panel is optional (Q10).

**Today.**
- `main`: four scripted bots with a fear model, and Claude able to play a shift through the test
  environment (`tools/eval/llm_agent.py`).
- 0.4.0 (merged as code on 14 November, nothing trained yet): the bots get a trained mind (Unity
  ML-Agents), a persona of 14 numbers, habits, moods, grudges and dice, and a scorecard of how
  human they are ([0.4.0 plan §7](0.4.0-build-plan.md#7-part-3-the-players)).
- **What adapts today:** only scripted layers on top of the trained mind. Confidence drops after a
  catch, a grudge keeps it away from where it was caught, a habit memory repeats its routes. The
  network itself has no memory and learns nothing about her.
- **The results so far:** the bots are what her prediction model learns escapes from (its
  destination models lost), and they almost never get away.

**The options, re-read for "realistic and adaptable"** (the sums are on the [agents page](0.5.0/agents.md)):

| Option | Adaptable? | Close to reality? | Fast and cheap enough to train her against? |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A+. 0.4.0's trained bots, made adaptable (my pick)** | yes: a memory in the network, and the bot's own record of her | fitted to real shifts, yours first | yes |
| B. Language-model players (Claude) | by reasoning, slowly | reasons like a person; no hand–eye skill | no: about $5,000 even on Haiku 5.5, and months |
| C. A hybrid: Claude sets goals, the trained mind walks | yes | a person's goals, a bot's body | no: it costs money every shift |
| D. Claude writes personas between shifts | no | more variety | yes |
| E. Copies of people (behaviour cloning) | only as the people did | the most | once there are about 100 players |

**What "adaptable" would mean** (Q38):

| Kind | What the bot does | How | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| Within a shift | remembers what it saw and heard of her this shift | a memory inside its network (recurrent memory, which ML-Agents supports) | it heard her by the freezers 30 s ago, so it takes the long way to the bins |
| Across shifts | keeps its own record of her: where she waits, which tricks she uses on it, what got it caught, where it got away | her Ledger turned round, read by its network (in-context adaptation) | she ambushed it at the clock twice, so in shift 4 it goes round by the other door |

**What "close to reality" would mean:** a new measure on the scorecard, *adaptation*: does a
bot's survival improve across a career against a fixed Karen, the way a person's does? Its target
comes from your own shifts first, then from playtesters. "Bot or human" (a classifier trying to
tell their shifts apart) stays the honest check.

**Why it matters for her:** a Karen trained against bots that adapt has to keep changing her
approach, which is what she'll face with people; and better escapes give her prediction model
something to learn from.

**It's ML,** so the adaptable bots go in the Karen ML PR, with Phase 3 and the layouts' training
half, after your approval (Y3.1). The optional panel (C2.1–C2.5) is test tooling, in its own PR.

**"Would opening a new project with all the improvements in graphics and assets take a lot of

|---|---|---|
| `Assets/` (models, textures, sounds) | 161 MB | 1–3 GB: higher-resolution textures and more models |

**"Can I save Unity projects on a flash card?"** You can, but not on a USB stick or SD card:

| Drive | Works? | Why |
|---|---|---|
| USB stick or SD card | possible, painfully slow | Unity reads and writes tens of thousands of small files in `Library/`; cheap flash is slow at that and wears out |
| **An external SSD (USB-C)** | **yes** | fast enough to work from; format it APFS for the Mac |
| A drive shared with the PC | for moving files only | the format both can read (exFAT) is fine for copying, less safe to work from |

My suggestion: keep the project on the Mac, and move the old builds and test data (about 3.9 GB)
to the PC or an external SSD (Y7.1). That roughly doubles the free space.

Windows, no.

| | Minimum | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **Mac** | **an Apple Silicon Mac (M1 or later) with 8 GB**, like yours | it's the base of every Apple Silicon Mac; Intel Macs are left out (too slow for this renderer) |
| **Windows** | to be set by a test on a modest PC | your PC (an i9 and an RTX 4090) is far above any minimum, and an M2's numbers don't carry over to Windows parts. A friend's older laptop, or a cheap cloud PC for an hour, sets the real floor |

## 17. Honest limits

- **Nothing here is tested.** The layouts were checked by a script on a 2.5 m drawing, not in
  Unity; the costs of language-model players are estimates from token counts, until C2.4 measures
  them.
- **Escapes are rare.** She loses a bot in a chase in about 5–13 shifts of 144. The escape table
  fills slowly, and its test needs players that get away (C3.5).
- **The destination models lost in 0.4.0.** Until the defect is found, the after-she-loses-you
  model is P1, the fitted hand step.
- **New layouts mean new stuck spots.** The bots got stuck on today's store (12 of 240 shifts in
  the 12 November baseline); every layout will need its own check (C4.6, C4.8).
- **Today's store couldn't be measured on the same drawing,** so the layouts' numbers compare with
  each other, not with today's.
- **Four shifts leave her little time to learn you.** She meets you in shifts 2–4, and the store
  changes under both of you at shifts 3 and 4; what carries over is your habits by kind of place.
  Her adaptation tests move to shifts 2–4, with fewer pairs than 0.4.0 planned.
- **The Windows minimum is untested:** your PC is far above any minimum, and no modest PC has
  been tried.
- **Ten layouts aren't many** for training across layouts; the held-out two will say whether
  she generalises, not prove it.
- **The styles are a quick pass**, like everything before the redesign. The choice made in the
  Look Lab is a direction, not the final art.
- **A language-model player isn't a person.** It reads prose, not pixels, and has no hand–eye
  skill.

| Term | What it is | How Kehai uses it | Real-world example |
|---|---|---|---|
| Flash sale | a sudden, short sale announced in the store | her new trick: more shoppers, more work, more eyes | a supermarket tannoy: "two for one in aisle 7" |
| Language-model agent | a program that uses a model like Claude to decide what to do, one step at a time | the agents panel: Claude plays shifts through the test environment | an AI assistant booking a table by clicking through a website |
| Hybrid agent | a slow planner that sets goals and a fast part that carries them out | option C of item 2: Claude sets the goal, the trained bot walks it | a satnav planning the route while you drive |
| Behaviour cloning | training a model to copy recorded players | copying people, later (about 100 players) | a self-driving car trained on hours of human driving |
| Held-out panel | test players nobody trains or tunes against | the language-model players, like the cautious bot | a blind taste test with a panel that never saw the recipe |
| Escape habits | counts of which way someone went after being lost from sight | the new Ledger table in item 3 | a fishing guide remembering where a fish runs when hooked |
| Lattice | a grid of square cells whose edges can be walls | the 5 m cells every layout is drawn on, as in the endless maze | the squares of graph paper you draw a maze on |
| Pillar (in the shelf kit) | the piece that joins shelf runs at an end, a corner or a crossing | an end cap, an L, a T or a cross at every joint | the corner posts of flat-pack shelving |
| Chokepoint | a place whose loss cuts an area in two | counted in every layout; she blocks these first | a single bridge into a town |
| Pocket (dead end) | a stretch you can enter only one way | counted in every layout; a place to be cornered | a cul-de-sac |
| Domain randomisation | training on many versions of a world, so the learner doesn't memorise one | a layout per career in training | robots trained in simulations with random lighting and floors, so they cope with a real room |
| Edition | one build of the game with a chosen set of features | Training, Testing, Gameplay from one project | the free and paid versions of the same app |
| Run register | a list of every training and test run, with what went in | `runs.jsonl`, the source of the reports and the status page | a lab notebook |
| Saliency | which inputs pushed a model's choice the most | the web report's "top three inputs" beside the network | a photo app highlighting the part of a picture it recognised a dog from |
| Post-processing | effects applied to the finished picture: colour, grain, glow, blur | the styles' filters in the Look Lab | a photo filter on Instagram |
| Colour grading | giving a whole picture a colour mood | each style's grade | the teal-and-orange look of action films |
| Dashboard | one page of numbers and charts, kept current | the status page of item 9 | a car's dashboard, or a fitness app's weekly summary |
| Benchmark | a fixed set of tests, run the same way on every version, so scores compare over time | every version of Karen plays the same players, layouts and seeds (item 5) | a car's crash-test rating, run the same way on every model |
| Recurrent memory | a network that keeps a little state from one step to the next, so it remembers what it saw | the bots remember where they last saw or heard her within a shift (C2.7) | your phone's keyboard guessing the next word from the sentence so far |
| In-context adaptation | adapting by reading a record, not by retraining | the bots read their own record of her (C2.6), as she reads your Ledger | a music app that changes its picks from what you skipped today, with the same model for everyone |
| Chiller | an open-fronted shop fridge, or a glass-door freezer, on the shop floor | 78 along the back wall; her Fog trick floods a room from its fridges | the milk and yoghurt fridges along a supermarket's back wall |
