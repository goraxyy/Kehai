## 6. Item 1: the PA flood, and more tricks

**Today.**
- **Shoppers:** at most 5 in the store in code (`CustomerSpawner.MaxCustomers`); the scene sets
  `maxActiveCustomers: 2` (read from the scene file on 13 November). So "the limit becomes 4" is
  exactly double.
- **The PA:** her announcements (`PaSystem.cs`), and the PA box in the staff room that switches
  it off (`PaBox.cs`). 0.4.1 adds the PA microphone beside the time clock (`ClosingMic.cs` on
  `claude/0-4-1-2305a2`): you press E there to announce closing, and customers keep coming until
  you do. 0.4.1 also makes the backstreet fixes 20 s holds, with a hold bar.
- **Her tricks:** 15 live ones on `main`, plus 0.4.1's Camera watch and Camera break. On
  7 November you removed five PA tricks (PA decoy, PA: your position, your task list, countdown,
  her footsteps). No reason was written down, which is [Q7](#16-open-questions).
- **The rules it must keep:** rule 10, no new chore once the doors close; a shift can always be
  won; every trick has a tell.

**The trick (my design; the full spec is on the [tricks page](0.5.0/tricks.md)).** "Flash sale":
the PA chimes and she announces a sale ("Two for one in aisle 7, for the next ten minutes!").
From then on up to 4 shoppers come in, twice as often. More shoppers means a longer queue, more
spills, more questions, and more people who can tell her where you are. **The fix:** hold E for
20 s at the PA microphone ("Hold E to call off the sale"); the shoppers already inside finish
their shopping, and the flow drops back to 2. It's a building trick, like Blackout: she doesn't
need to be there, but the PA must be on.

**Where the fix happens:**

| Option | Pros | Cons |
|---|---|---|
| **A. Hold E at the 0.4.1 PA microphone (my pick)** | one place for everything said on the PA; it's beside the clock, so you know the way; same 20 s hold as the backstreet | needs 0.4.1 merged; the staff room is also where you clock out, so she can wait there |
| B. Hold E at the PA box | exists on `main` | the box already means "PA off": one object, two meanings |
| C. No chore: the sale ends after 90 s | the simplest | not what you asked; her learner can't see how fast you answer |

**More tricks.** Six ideas on the [tricks page](0.5.0/tricks.md), each built on something
0.4.0 or 0.4.1 added: **False trail** (she makes a noise in one place, then waits where the
mirror map says you think she isn't), **Screen loop** (she freezes one office camera picture on
an empty aisle), **Room lights** (she switches one room's lights off; you switch them on at the
wall), **Freezer alarm** (a door alarm covers every footstep in the cold room, hers and yours),
**The complaint** (a shopper she spoke to waits at the till for 20 s of your time), **Door jam**
(an automatic door stuck open or shut until reset).

## 7. Item 2: bots into agents

**Discuss first.**

**Today.**
- `main`: four scripted bots with a fear model, and **Claude playing a shift** through the test
  environment (`tools/eval/llm_agent.py`): it reads the store as prose, picks one of 17 actions,
  and the real player body does it. It's set to `claude-opus-5`.
- 0.4.0 (branch): the bots get a trained mind (Unity ML-Agents), a persona of 14 numbers,
  habits, moods and dice, so they're recognisable but never the same twice; a scorecard measures
  how human they are.
- **What the new results add:** the bots are what the prediction model learns from, and its
  destination models lost; and the bots almost never get away from her. Better, more varied
  players matter more than they did.

**What "agent" could mean.** Five options; the sums are on the [agents page](0.5.0/agents.md).

| Option | Speed (a 150 s shift) | Money | Realism |
|---|---|---|---|
| A. 0.4.0's bots (trained mind + persona) | 15–30 s in batch | none | varied, but bot habits |
| B. A language-model player (Claude, as `llm_agent.py`) | minutes: one model call per action, 2–10 s each | about $0.05 a shift on Haiku 5.5, $0.50–1 on Sonnet 5.5, $1–2 on Opus 5.5 | reasons about goals like a person; no hand–eye skill; reads prose, not pixels |
| C. A hybrid: the language model sets the goal every 20–30 s, the trained mind and reflexes do the rest | about a minute | about a fifth of B | the best mix, and the most work |
| D. A language model writes the bots' personas and goals between shifts | as A | pennies | more variety, same bot bodies |
| E. Copy people (behaviour cloning from replays) | as A | none | the most human; needs about 100 players |

**My recommendation.** A stays the opponent she trains against (only A and E are fast and cheap
enough for millions of decisions). Add **B as a held-out panel**: a few fixed language-model
players (Haiku 5.5 for volume, a few Sonnet 5.5 shifts) that no part of Karen ever trains or
tunes against, reported beside the scripted bots, the trained bots and you. It tests whether she
generalises to a mind that isn't hers or the bots', it gives the prediction model escapes it
never trained on, and "a fair AI tested against a language-model agent" is a strong showpiece
for a behaviour-engine company. C can come later if B shows the panel is useful.

## 8. Item 3: cheat training

**Discuss first.** This is Karen ML: planned and approved, then built in the one Karen ML PR.

**Today.**
- **0.4.0's prediction model is already the "after she loses you" model.** It's supervised:
  every moment she can't see you is an example, and the answer is where you really were. It's
  fitted centrally, never on your computer (0.4.0's rule D5,
  [§4.1](0.4.0-build-plan.md#41-defaults-i-picked-change-any)), and people's shifts reach it
  through the round routine after playtest v2
  ([Phase 7 there](0.4.0-build-plan.md#phase-7-when-real-players-arrive-c)).
- **Your Ledger already learns from where you really went,** between shifts: the Director, which
  is allowed to know where you are, feeds it your dwell, hiding and routes
  (`KarenLedger.ObservePlayer`). But a route only closes at a job (a spill, a bin, coffee, the
  clock). **Nothing records where you went after she lost you.**
- **The results so far:** the fitted hand step (P1) won; the destination models lost; and
  escapes are rare (she loses a bot in a chase in 5–13 of 144 shifts).

**What "cheat training" can mean, plainly:**

| Option | Where it runs | What changes | Keeps 0.4.0's rule? | Pros | Cons |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| A. A central refit | the Windows PC, from uploaded sessions | the prediction model's numbers, aimed at the moments after she loses someone | yes: it's the round routine, focused | everyone gains; tested on held-out players; ships through the signed pack | needs playtest v2 and players; weeks between refits |
| B. A count on your computer | your Ledger, at each shift's end | a table: from each place she lost you, which way you went; it fades like the rest | yes: counting, as the Ledger does today | works from your second shift; nothing is trained; tiny | few examples, since she rarely loses you |
| C. A fit on your computer | your computer, after your last shift | your own copy of the model's numbers | **no**: it breaks D5 | the most personal | slow on a laptop; no held-out test of your copy; the pack's gate can't check it |
| **D. A and B (my pick)** | both | both | yes | everyone's model improves, and she knows your escapes too | two parts to build |

**Why "cheat" is fair here.** Within a shift she still only knows what she senses. Between
shifts she remembers where you really went, as the Ledger already does. The fairness test is
that the player can see it: the shift report could say "she's learned where you run when she
loses you" ([Q13](#16-open-questions)).

**What the lost destination models mean for it.** Option B doesn't need them: her route guesses
(Cut, Ambush, the Hunt's run) and P1 read the escape table directly. Option A refits P1 first,
and P2 only once its defect is found (a 0.4.0 follow-up).

**Fits either answer to Q1.** B trains nothing. A runs whenever a refit runs: now, or once on
the final game.

## 17. Honest limits

- **Nothing here is tested.** The layouts were checked by a script on a 2.5 m drawing, not in
  Unity; the costs of language-model players are estimates from token counts, until C2.4 measures
  them.
- **Escapes are rare.** She loses a bot in a chase in about 5–13 shifts of 144. The escape table
  fills slowly, and its test needs players that get away (C3.5).
- **The destination models lost in 0.4.0.** Until the defect is found, the after-she-loses-you
  model is P1, the fitted hand step.
- **New layouts mean new stuck spots.** The bots got stuck on today's store (12 of 240 shifts in
  the 12 November baseline); every layout will need its own check (C4.6, C4.8).
- **Ten layouts aren't many** for training across layouts; the held-out two will say whether
  she generalises, not prove it.
- **The styles are a quick pass**, like everything before the redesign. The choice made in the
  Look Lab is a direction, not the final art.
- **A language-model player isn't a person.** It reads prose, not pixels, and has no hand–eye
  skill.

## 19. Terms

| Term | What it is | How Kehai uses it | Real-world example |
|---|---|---|---|
| Flash sale | a sudden, short sale announced in the store | her new trick: more shoppers, more work, more eyes | a supermarket tannoy: "two for one in aisle 7" |
| Language-model agent | a program that uses a model like Claude to decide what to do, one step at a time | the agents panel: Claude plays shifts through the test environment | an AI assistant booking a table by clicking through a website |
| Hybrid agent | a slow planner that sets goals and a fast part that carries them out | option C of item 2: Claude sets the goal, the trained bot walks it | a satnav planning the route while you drive |
| Behaviour cloning | training a model to copy recorded players | copying people, later (about 100 players) | a self-driving car trained on hours of human driving |
| Held-out panel | test players nobody trains or tunes against | the language-model players, like the cautious bot | a blind taste test with a panel that never saw the recipe |
| Escape habits | counts of which way someone went after being lost from sight | the new Ledger table in item 3 | a fishing guide remembering where a fish runs when hooked |
| Lattice | a grid of square cells whose edges can be walls | the 5 m cells every layout is drawn on, as in the endless maze | the squares of graph paper you draw a maze on |
| Pillar (in the shelf kit) | the piece that joins shelf runs at an end, a corner or a crossing | an end cap, an L, a T or a cross at every joint | the corner posts of flat-pack shelving |
| Chokepoint | a place whose loss cuts an area in two | counted in every layout; she blocks these first | a single bridge into a town |
| Pocket (dead end) | a stretch you can enter only one way | counted in every layout; a place to be cornered | a cul-de-sac |
| Domain randomisation | training on many versions of a world, so the learner doesn't memorise one | a layout per career in training | robots trained in simulations with random lighting and floors, so they cope with a real room |
| Edition | one build of the game with a chosen set of features | Training, Testing, Gameplay from one project | the free and paid versions of the same app |
| Run register | a list of every training and test run, with what went in | `runs.jsonl`, the source of the reports and the status page | a lab notebook |
| Saliency | which inputs pushed a model's choice the most | the training viewer's "top three inputs" | a photo app highlighting the part of a picture it recognised a dog from |
| Post-processing | effects applied to the finished picture: colour, grain, glow, blur | the styles' filters in the Look Lab | a photo filter on Instagram |
| Colour grading | giving a whole picture a colour mood | each style's grade | the teal-and-orange look of action films |
| Dashboard | one page of numbers and charts, kept current | the status page of item 9 | a car's dashboard, or a fitness app's weekly summary |
