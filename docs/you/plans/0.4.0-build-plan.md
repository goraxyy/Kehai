## 2. The short version

```
                 paid for                              pacing rules (the Director)
  Vital          catching you; sooner is better        off (the fairness rules stay)
  Basic          your fear near its target, and a      on
                 catch at the right moment
                 └── same senses, same tricks, same body, same network shape ──┘

  people's shifts ─┬─► the players (bots) ─► training in simulation ─► Vital, Basic ─┐
                   ├─► the prediction model (where people go next) ─────────────────┤─► the general
                   ├─► her senses' numbers, the starting Ledger ("everyone") ───────┘  model: in the
                   └─► judging only (held-out players)                                 build, and sent
                                                                                       out between
                                                                                       builds (§6.5)

  your shifts ─────► your Ledger (on your computer, yours only) ─► read by Basic and by the
                                                                   prediction model as you play
```

- **One body, two brains.** Her senses, belief map, tricks and fairness rules don't change. Only
  the part that answers "what do I do next?" is trained, twice, with two different rewards.
- **The brain reads your Ledger; it is never retrained on your computer.** That's the separation:
  the trained parts are the same for everyone, and your Ledger is yours alone.
- **People teach the bots; the bots teach Karen.** A recorded shift can't be replayed with a
  different Karen, so real sessions can't train the brain directly. They shape the bots, set the
  Ledger's starting values, train the prediction model, and judge the result.
- **The bots get a trained mind of their own:** a persona (14 numbers), habits, moods, and choices
  drawn with dice, so they're recognisable but never identical.
- **PR #68 becomes "where she thinks you're going".** She guesses your destination first (a job,
  one of her chores, the clock, a hiding spot), then the walk. One model feeds her belief map, her
  hunt, where she puts her tricks, and both brains.
- **Two smaller learned parts sit under it:** how far to trust each sense, and a second map of
  where you think she is.
- **The general model keeps improving between builds.** A new one goes out as a small data file,
  only after it beats the current one on players it never trained on. The build's own copy is
  the fallback.
- **Everything is judged the way the repo already demands:** seeded pairs, standard errors,
  held-out players, metrics written down first, negative results reported.

## 4. Decisions

### 4.1 Defaults I picked (change any)

| # | Decision | Why |
|---|---|---|
| D1 | **The trained part picks the action and the trick together** ("Hunt with Cut", "Flush with Blackout"): about 30 pairs. Today's rung F picks only the trick | 58% of 0.2.0's catches came in Hunt and 39% in Check. A brain that can't choose *when* to hunt can't be catch-oriented |
| D2 | **Two separate brains with the same shape,** not one brain with a dial | scaring and catching need different habits; each stays simple to test. Same shape means one pipeline |
| D3 | **PPO through Unity ML-Agents,** warm-started by copying the planner | it's already set up and smoke-tested (`tools/rl/`); copying first means training starts at today's level, not from random |
| D4 | **Vital: pacing off. Basic: pacing on** (the Director's shield) | Basic's breathing room is then a guarantee in code, not something she might unlearn |
| D5 | **Per-player adaptation is the Ledger, which the trained parts read.** No training on the player's computer | the game can't train a network at play time, and this keeps "everyone" and "you" in two separate places |
| D6 | **Trained parts ship as generated C# with a model card,** run by small C# code | the same choice PR #68 made: no binary in git, no package needed to play, every copy of the project compiles |
| D7 | **Her belief goes in as a short list** (mass per area, her three best guesses), not the 32×32 picture | no picture means a much smaller network: faster to train, trivial to run in C# |
| D8 | **Bots = scripted reflexes + a trained mind + a persona + habits;** one network plays every persona | reflexes stay honest (fear always shows); the mind gives variety |
| D9 | **A league in rounds,** one side learning while the others are frozen | simpler to debug than both learning at once, and it works for bots whose goal isn't just "beat Karen" |
| D10 | **Real sessions teach the bots, the prediction model and the starting Ledger, and judge.** They don't train the brain directly | PPO learns by trying things; a recording can't be re-tried |
| D11 | **Vital is a benchmark and a sparring partner for now,** not a menu option | a hard mode can come later for free: it's the same file format |
| D12 | **Each brain is trained twice,** from two random starts | RL results vary from run to run; one lucky run isn't a result |
| D13 | **PR #68 is redesigned around destinations** and built here, before the brains train | 0.2.0 and 0.3.0 changed what there is to predict ([§8.2](#82-why-redesign-it)), and the brains must train on the belief they'll play with |
| D14 | **The Ledger blends "everyone" and "you" by evidence, per trick and per kind of trick** (taken from PR #76) | she uses about 3.6 tricks a shift (0.3.0's ablation): far too few to learn each trick's effect on you alone |
| D15 | **The general model can update between builds** (added 11 November). The same numbers as D6's generated C# also travel as a signed **model pack**, a data file the game fetches at launch and uses only if it passes its checks. D6's copy in the build stays as the floor. **Publishing waits for your yes each time.** The quick fits may later run on a schedule; a refresh round of the brains stays on your go | you asked for a central model that keeps improving. The risk is a bad model reaching everyone at once, so there's a gate, versions and rollback. The rules and the mask are code, so a pack can make her dull or too deadly, never unfair |
| D16 | **Her senses' numbers are fitted, not hand-set** (added 11 November): the same formulas, their numbers learned through the filter. They're fitted first and held the same in every prediction arm | cheap, because the trace and the replay are built anyway; and it makes her percentages honest |
| D17 | **A second map: where you think she is** (added 11 November), built only from what she knows. A small filter with a few fitted numbers, not a network | people steer by where they *think* she is. This gives the prediction model, the brains and her trick placement that view |

### 4.3 Against PR #76: pros and cons

Both want the same thing: Karen learns from everyone, and adapts to you. They get there differently.

| | PR #76 ("Karen learns from everyone") | 0.4.0 |
|---|---|---|
| What learns | a statistical model: what each trick does to your fear (and to the chance of a catch), by situation | a network that picks the action and the trick; the Ledger; the prediction model |
| How | a fit from logged shifts (Bayesian regression), picking by Thompson sampling | PPO in simulation, plus two fits from logged shifts |
| The two Karens | no. They stay an idea; its catch estimate, at weight 0, leaves room for a dial later | yes: two trained brains |
| What she can learn | which trick, inside an action the hand-set scores chose | when to hunt, which trick, and what to chain |
| People's shifts | train it directly: every logged trick is an example | reach the brain through the bots; reach the starting Ledger and the prediction model directly |
| "You" | a proper blend of everyone and you, updated after every trick, with its own uncertainty | the Ledger (with that blend, D14), read by a brain that was trained to use it |
| The bots | today's four scripted profiles | trained, varied players |
| Where you'll be (PR #68) | left as its own PR | redesigned and built in |
| Computer time | minutes, plus one bot run of a few hours | about a month of Mac nights (estimate) |
| Tasks | about 20 | 57 |
| Needs real players | to matter at all: that's its point | no for the build; yes for the last phase |

**PR #76**

| Pros | Cons |
|---|---|
| Cheap: fits in minutes on this Mac | Doesn't train the two Karens you asked for |
| Learns from people's shifts directly | Can't learn when to hunt, or sequences: it only ranks tricks |
| Sound per player: blends everyone and you by evidence, and tries unsure tricks on purpose | Its tests still run on four scripted bots |
| Easy to read: "Blackout: everyone +0.12, you +0.01" | Does little until playtest v2 has players |
| Low risk to how the game feels: the Director and the action choice don't change | The rungs it improves on (D, E) didn't beat the planner; it may not either |
| | Written for two builders, and it leaves PR #68 aside |

**0.4.0**

| Pros | Cons |
|---|---|
| Does what you asked: two trained models, with their rewards and tests | Expensive: weeks of nights against minutes |
| The brain decides when to hunt and what to chain, which is where catching and pacing are really decided | Riskier: RL can learn to exploit bots, and may not beat the planner |
| Builds varied, trained players, needed for an honest test of any learner | People's shifts reach the brain only through the bots |
| One "everyone / you" design across the brain, the Ledger and the prediction model | Harder to say *why* she picked something |
| Works before any real player exists | Much more code to build and keep |
| The stronger showpiece: a league, adaptation by reading, a redesigned belief model | The personal part is only as good as the bots' variety in training |

**What 0.4.0 takes from PR #76:**
- the learning records, with the chance of each pick;
- splits by player, and one player, one vote;
- a model card beside every generated file;
- everyone's habits as the Ledger's start;
- **the blend of everyone and you by evidence** (D14, [§6.2](#62-how-basic-adapts-to-you-without-retraining));
- its offline check, as a reported number: does the starting Ledger predict held-out players' reactions better than the hand-set guesses?

**My recommendation:** 0.4.0, with those parts taken in. If the cost worries you, there's a
cheaper middle path: PR #76's learner with its catch dial gives two Karens as two *settings* of one
model, with no trained brain and no new bots. I'd guess about a week of building. It's a smaller
result, and it can still be built later as a fallback if Basic doesn't beat today's game.

## 5. Part 1: the two Karens

### 5.1 What's the same, what differs

| | Vital | Basic |
|---|---|---|
| Senses, belief map, prediction model, tricks, body | the same | the same |
| Never knows where you really are (rules 1, 2) | yes. The Director's hint toward your area is off, so every catch is her own | yes. The hint stays as today, and windows where it fired are flagged in the data |
| Hands-on tricks at her hands, shift stays winnable, no chore after closing (rules 5, 6, 9, 10) | yes | yes |
| She never chases you outside the store | yes | yes |
| The Director's pacing: tiers, tension budget, cooldowns, the 45 s recovery (rule 4) | **off** | **on** |
| Reads your Ledger | no. She plays everyone the same | **yes** |
| Paid for | catching you, sooner is better | fear near its target, a catch at the right moment |
| For | a benchmark ("how good can a fair AI get"), a sparring partner for the bots, a hard mode later | the game |

### 5.2 What the trained part decides

Today, in three steps:

```
1. which action?  (Roam, Check, Search, Hunt, Flush, Disrupt…)   hand-set scores      ActionSet.Choose
2. which trick?   (Blackout, Spill, Cut…)                        the Ledger's picker   HtnPlanner.Rank
3. the steps      (walk there, do it, leave)                     the planner           Tactic.Plan
```

In 0.4.0 the brain answers 1 and 2 as one choice, and 3 stays as it is:

- **When she's asked:** only when the planner would re-decide anyway: a plan ended, an interrupt
  fired, or the minimum commit time has passed. "Finish what she starts" (0.3.0) still holds.
- **What she may pick (the mask):** only pairs that can really run now. For Basic the Director
  has already removed what pacing forbids. For Vital only the fairness rules and physics remove
  anything.
- **How she picks:** the network gives a chance to each legal pair, and she draws one. She
  doesn't always take the top one, so she's less predictable, and the exact chance of each pick
  is logged (needed to judge a new brain on old data).
- **If the brain's pick can't be planned,** the planner's own order is the fallback, as on rung F today.

*Example.* She has lost you near the freezers; the doors close in 90 s.
- Legal for Basic (tension is low, a hunt ended 40 s ago): Search, Check, Flush with Phantom
  chime, Lie in wait with Ambush.
- Legal for Vital: all of those, plus Hunt and Flush with Blackout.

### 5.3 What she sees

Only what Karen may know. The code lives under `Decision/`, where the existing source scan
(`BodyBeliefAndDecisions_NeverReadThePlayer`) forbids reading the player.

| Group | Examples | New? |
|---|---|---|
| Her appraisal | how sure she is, how stale, panic, the target, tension, task load | today |
| The shift | phase, time left, doors open or closed, chores open | partly new |
| Each trick | cooldown left, uses this shift | today |
| Her belief | mass per area | new (replaces the picture) |
| Where you're going | her three best guesses: what kind (a job, her chore, the clock, an exit, a hiding spot), how far, how strong ([§8](#8-part-4-where-she-thinks-youre-going)) | new |
| What you know of her | how long since you probably sensed her, how far she is from where you think she is ([§8.8](#88-where-you-think-she-is)) | new |
| **Your Ledger** (Basic only; zeros for Vital) | per trick and per kind of trick: her blended guess for you, and how much of it is you; her best place for it; your habits (sprint share, hiding, routes) | new |

### 5.4 What each is paid for

A shift is one episode. In test runs a catch ends the shift.

**Vital**

| Part | Amount |
|---|---|
| A catch | +1, less the share of the shift that had passed |
| No catch | −1, however the shift ends |
| A fairness violation | −0.1 each (the mask should make this impossible; it's counted anyway) |
| Your fear | nothing |

*Example, a 300 s shift:* caught at 60 s → 1 − 60/300 = **+0.8**. Caught at 270 s → **+0.1**. You
clock out, or the shift times out → **−1**.

**Basic**

| Part | Amount | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Pacing | −\|panic − target\| per second ÷ shift length: between −1 and 0 over a shift | the Director's own job, as rung F is paid today. Too calm costs as much as too scared |
| The catch dial | `w` for a catch in a tense phase (Spike or Crunch), 0 otherwise | `w` starts at +0.15 and is turned between rounds until her catch rate sits in your target (Q2). It can go below zero |
| Sameness | −0.02 for each use of a trick past its third in a shift | so she doesn't lean on one trick |
| A stuck shift | −0.5 if the shift can't end | 0.3.0's first ablation had 13 of these |
| A fairness violation | −0.1 each | as today |

*Example:* she kept your fear 0.12 from the target on average (−0.12), caught you in Crunch
(+0.15), and used Spill five times (−0.04): **−0.01**.

### 5.5 How a trained brain gets into the game

1. Training writes checkpoints to `tools/rl/results/` (not in git).
2. `tools/learn/export_brain.py` turns one into generated C#: `KarenBrainVital.g.cs`,
   `KarenBrainBasic.g.cs`. Tens of thousands of numbers each.
3. Each file has a **model card:** what it was trained against, for how long, its validation
   scores, and the command that made it.
4. A small C# network (`BrainNet.cs`) runs it. The game needs neither ML-Agents nor a model file.
5. **A parity test:** on 1,000 recorded situations the C# network and the trainer's own model
   give the same chances (to 4 decimal places).
6. **Outputs are tied to names** (`flush:blackout`), not positions. A removed trick is ignored. A
   new trick has no output yet, so the planner ranks it until the next training.
7. `-karen-brain planner|basic|vital` chooses which brain runs. Rung F now means Basic.
8. The same numbers are also written into the **model pack** ([§6.5](#65-when-a-new-general-model-ships)).
   Vital isn't in the pack (D11).

### 5.6 How she could cheat the reward, and the guard

RL optimises the number, not the intent. The known risks, written before any training:

| Risk | What it looks like | Guard |
|---|---|---|
| **Clock camping** (Vital) | she waits at the time clock: you must come back to it, and after closing it's almost the only place left to go | report the share of catches within 10 m of the clock. Above 50%, add a rule (no waiting inside the staff room) and retrain |
| **Cornering** (Basic) | freezing counts as panic, so trapping you scores without scaring you | the winnable rule (6), the stuck-shift cost, and a count of "frozen with her in the doorway" |
| **Coasting** (Basic) | she does nothing while the target is low | the pacing cost is two-sided |
| **One trick** | an unshielded smoke policy once picked Follow 467 times in a shift | Basic's cooldowns stay on; the sameness cost; the trick mix is reported |
| **Bot exploits** | a move that reliably steers a bot does nothing to a person | many personas, old opponents kept, the held-out players, the exploit check ([§11](#11-how-its-judged)), and in the end real players |
| **A lucky run** | one training run looks great | two runs each (D12); checkpoints are chosen on validation seeds, never test seeds |
| **Leaked truth** | an input that secretly depends on where you are | the source scan, plus a new test: move the player where she can't sense it, and her inputs must not change |

## 6. Part 2: everyone, and you

### 6.1 Two stores

| | The general model ("everyone") | Your Ledger ("you") |
|---|---|---|
| What | the trained brain, the prediction model, her senses' numbers, and the **starting Ledger:** what each trick does to people on average, where people hide, the routes they take | what she has seen of *you:* what each trick and each kind of trick did to you, which pairs worked, her best places, your routes and hiding spots, how fast you answer her chores, your calm baseline |
| Made from | bot careers, and every consenting player's shifts | your own shifts only |
| Made when | offline, never on your computer | while you play: after every trick's 20 s window, and at each shift's end |
| Where | generated C# in the build, or the newest model pack ([§6.5](#65-when-a-new-general-model-ships)); identical for everyone on the same version | a file on your computer (`karen_ledger.json`) |
| Changes | with a new build or a new pack, never during a shift | all the time; it fades each shift (rule 7) |
| Size | tens of thousands of numbers | a few hundred |
| Wipe | — | wiping it gives you "everyone's" Karen again |

### 6.2 How Basic adapts to you without retraining

**The blend** (D14, from PR #76). Her guess for you is everyone's number and yours, weighted by
how much she has seen of you:

```
her guess = (n × yours + 2 × everyone's) ÷ (n + 2)        n = times she has seen it on you
```

It's kept twice: per trick, and per **kind** of trick (what it attacks: sight, work, sound, trust,
space, social, you; `Tactic.Attacks`). A blackout that barely moved you also lowers her guess for
the other tricks that attack sight, so a few tricks a shift still teach her something.

**Reading it.** The brain was trained on thousands of bot careers. Each career had its own hidden
persona and its own Ledger, filling up over six shifts. So the brain was paid for *using* a Ledger
well. In the game the brain never changes. Your Ledger does, and she reads it at every decision.

*An illustration (the numbers are made up):*

| Moment | Everyone | Yours | Her guess | What Basic does |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Your first shift | Blackout +0.12 | nothing yet | +0.12 | tries Blackout early |
| After 3 blackouts that barely moved you | +0.12 | +0.01 over 3 | +0.05 | Blackout's chance drops; Fog's too, a little (same kind) |
| Shift 3: you always reset the breakers by the short way | — | that route, 4 times | — | Blackout, then Ambush on that route |
| You change your route for two shifts | — | the old route fades | — | she stops waiting there (rule 7) |

### 6.3 How sessions become training

```
your shift ─► the session zip (if you agreed) ─► R2 ─► the dataset, one folder per player
                                                          │
     ┌───────────────────┬──────────────────┬─────────────┴──────┬────────────────────┐
  the starting       the prediction     the bots'            held-out players     the scorecard
  Ledger             model              personas             (judging only)       (bots vs people)
  (a fit: minutes)   (a fit: minutes)   (fitted to people)
     │                   │                  │
     └───────────────────┴────────┬─────────┘
                    training in simulation ─► new Vital and Basic ─► the next build
```

What each amount of data unlocks:

| Players | Unlocks |
|---|---|
| 1 (you) | the first human reference for the bots' scorecard; a first check of the prediction model on a person |
| about 10 | persona ranges fitted to people; the prediction model refitted with people in it; the first honest "bots vs people" comparison |
| about 30 | the starting Ledger fitted from people, counted above bots; the Panic Index check (PR #76) |
| 100 or more | bots that copy real players directly; testing two brains on two halves of the testers. Not in 0.4.0 |

### 6.4 The rules that keep them apart

1. **One way only:** sessions → dataset → general model. A Ledger never becomes part of a
   trained model. A snapshot of it travels in the zip, and is used only for "where people hide".
2. **The general model is read-only while you play.** A test checks the game never writes it.
3. **Your Ledger holds only your evidence,** stored as plain counts and averages, not as
   differences from "everyone". The blend is worked out when she reads it, so a new general model
   doesn't make your Ledger wrong.
4. **Every learning record says what she knew:** her inputs (the Ledger's numbers included), the
   legal picks, her pick and its chance, the brain's version, and what happened next. The trainer
   can then tell "what she'd do for anyone" from "what she did because of your Ledger".
5. **Split by player, never by shift.** A fixed set of players (by their random ID) is kept for
   judging only. For bots: the cautious profile and the careful corner ([§7.6](#76-how-theyre-trained)).
6. **One player, one vote.** Each player counts the same in a fit, however many shifts they played.
7. **Forgetting works.** Wiping the Ledger resets her for you. Playtest v2's `forget <id>` removes
   a player from the dataset, and the next fit is made without them.
8. **"Everyone" is experience, not cheating.** It says "people often hide in the lockers", never
   "you're in locker 3".

### 6.5 When a new general model ships

> **Model pack.** *What:* a model's numbers sent as a data file, without a new version of the app.
> *In Kehai:* the general model, fetched when the game starts.
> *Elsewhere:* an antivirus downloading new definitions; your phone's keyboard getting a new
> language model without an app update.

A new general model reaches players two ways (D15):

| | In the build | A model pack |
|---|---|---|
| What | generated C# | one data file: Basic's brain, the prediction model, her senses' numbers, the mirror map's numbers and the starting Ledger, each with its card |
| When | each release | whenever a candidate passes the gate |
| For | the floor: the game always runs, with or without a connection | improving between releases |

```
 everyone's shifts ─► the dataset ─► the fits (minutes) and a refresh round (nights, your go)
                                              │
                                       a candidate pack
                                              │
               the gate: held-out players, fairness, catch rate ──✗──► kept as a report
                                              │ ✓, and your yes
                        published as version N+1; "current" points at it
                                              │
   the game, at launch: a newer pack? ─► checks it ─► uses it from the next shift
                        offline, or a check fails ─► the last good pack, else the build's own
```

- **The gate** (`tools/learn/publish.py`), on the held-out players, against the pack that's
  current. A candidate passes only if:
  - no part's primary metric ([§11](#11-how-its-judged)) is worse by more than one standard error;
  - at least one is better by more than two;
  - fairness violations are 0, and stuck shifts are no more than before;
  - Basic's catch rate is inside your target (Q2).
- **The game's own checks:** the signature (the public key is in the build; the private key is
  never in git), the inputs' version, every trick name known, a size limit. A pack that fails one
  is ignored and the reason is logged.
- **What a pack can't do.** The fairness rules, the mask and the Director are code. A bad pack can
  make her dull or too deadly. It can't make her unfair. Rolling back is pointing "current" at the
  version before.
- **The fetch says nothing about you:** a plain download, with no ID and no Ledger.
- **Never during a shift.** A new pack is used from the next shift.
- **In 0.4.0:** the pack, the loader, the checks and the gate are built, and tested from a local
  file (`-karen-pack <file>`). The server and the fetch wait for playtest v2
  ([Phase 7](#phase-7-when-real-players-arrive-c)).
- **Who starts what** (new on 11 November; change it if you want). Publishing waits for your yes
  each time. The quick fits can later run on a schedule. A refresh round of the brains is a long
  run, so it stays on your go.

*Example.* Forty players' shifts arrive in a week. The weekly fit finds people answer the stopped
clock much later than the bots do. The candidate's prediction score on the held-out players is
better by three standard errors, and nothing else moved. You say yes; version 7 goes out; the next
time anyone starts the game, she expects them at the clock later.

**Your Ledger across versions:**

- Your Ledger is kept. It's keyed by trick names, and the new brain reads it as the old one did.
- A trick that was removed drops out of the Ledger; a new trick starts from "everyone".
- The Ledger records which brain version last used it, for the shift record.

## 7. Part 3: the players

### 7.1 Four layers

| Layer | Made how | Does | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| Body | as today (`AgentDriver`) | walks, opens doors, mops: the real player's body and timings | — |
| Reflexes | scripted, set by the persona (`BotFear`) | fear, startles, freezing, dropping things, jumping back from her, sidestepping a throw | a jumpy one freezes 1–3 s on a big startle |
| **Choices** | **trained with ML-Agents** (behaviour `Employee`) | what to do next, how fast, which one | "there's a queue, but she was just in aisle 4: go round, and hurry" |
| Habits and mood | a small memory per career | favourite routes and hiding spots, job order, confidence | hides in Aisle 12 again; avoids the freezer for two shifts after dying there |

**Why reflexes stay scripted:** fear has to show in the body, because that's what her Panic Index
reads. A trained mind in charge of them would learn a poker face.

### 7.2 The persona: 14 numbers

Set once per career, each between 0 and 1.

| Trait | Low | High |
|---|---|---|
| Diligence | skips small jobs | every job; the queue first |
| Caution | walks right past her | keeps far away, hides early |
| Pace | walks | sprints everywhere |
| Habit | varies routes and spots | the same routes, the same hiding spot |
| Consistency | erratic | the same choice in the same situation |
| Attention | misses her sounds while busy | notices everything, reacts fast |
| Curiosity | ignores odd things | goes to look (the doors chiming, a noise) |
| Grudge | forgets where it was caught | avoids that place for shifts |
| Calming down | stays scared for a minute | shrugs it off in seconds |
| What scares them (5) | the dark · sounds and the PA · seeing her · her dashes · being chased | each from "nothing" to "a lot" |

- **The four scripted profiles are four points in this space.** Reckless, for example: pace 1,
  caution 0.1, only "being chased" scares them.
- **Why "what scares them" matters:** it makes tricks work differently on different players, which
  is exactly what Basic has to read from a Ledger.
- **The Survivor** is one extreme persona: all caution and attention, and it wins only by clocking
  out. It's Vital's sparring partner, not a realistic player.

### 7.3 What the trained mind sees, picks and is paid for

**Sees** (about 70 numbers, its own senses only, from `EnvWorld.Observe`; the existing test
`Players_NeverReadKaren` covers the new code):
- itself: energy, what it carries, its fear, time left;
- the jobs: how many of each kind, how far the nearest, the longest queue wait;
- her: in view or not, how far, which way, her eye colour, seconds since it last saw or heard her;
- the store: lights, fog, her chores waiting;
- its memory: where she was, where it was caught, its usual spots;
- its persona.

**Picks** (three small choices at once; impossible ones are masked):

| Choice | Options |
|---|---|
| What (14) | serve the till · help a shopper · restock · mop · bins · take the bag out · answer her chore · coffee · clock out · go round · back off · hide · stop and look · wait |
| How (3) | sneak · walk · hurry |
| Which (3) | the nearest · my usual · the safest (furthest from where she was) |

**When:** when an action ends or is cut short, on a startle, when she comes into view, when a new
chore appears; otherwise every 4 s at most. About 40–80 choices a shift.

**Every choice is logged with where it leads.** Those are the prediction model's training labels
([§8.5](#85-how-its-trained)).

**Paid for** (the persona sets the weights, so one network learns every character):

| Part | Weighted by |
|---|---|
| + a job done, + clocking out, − shoppers left waiting | diligence |
| − being caught | caution |
| − fear × seconds | what scares them |
| − seconds sprinting | (1 − pace) |
| + a little for the usual route or spot | habit |
| + a little for going to look at something odd, once | curiosity |

### 7.4 Patterns, but never the same shift twice

| Mechanism | Gives | Example |
|---|---|---|
| The persona, fixed for a career | a character | "always clears the tills first and never sprints" |
| Habit memory | choices that repeat, which her Ledger can learn | hides in Aisle 12 three shifts running |
| Dice: it draws from its chances instead of taking the top one; "consistency" sets how loaded they are | no two shifts alike | Aisle 12 70% of the time, the stockroom 20%, elsewhere 10% |
| Mood: confidence drops after a catch and comes back over quiet shifts | drift across a career | careful for two shifts after dying, then bolder again |
| Grudge: it remembers where she caught or found it | it adapts back at her | found in Aisle 12, it switches spot. This is what tests rule 7 |
| Attention and reaction time | human misses | hears the light flicker 6 times in 10 while serving |

### 7.5 How real players shape them

- **Trait fitting** (`tools/learn/fit_personas.py`): for each real player, measure what shows in
  their shifts (sprint share, hides a shift, how soon they back off, job order, how alike their
  routes are, how fast they answer each chore), then find the persona whose bot produces the same
  numbers. The personas' ranges for training are then set to cover the real players.
- **It starts with your own recorded shifts** (Q4).
- **Copying people directly** (behavioural cloning from replays) needs about 100 players and a
  converter from replays to choices. It's not in 0.4.0; the data format leaves room for it.

### 7.6 How they're trained

- **Warm start:** the scripted players' choices are recorded as demonstrations (efficient,
  skittish, reckless; never cautious), and the network copies them first.
- **Three lessons** (ML-Agents' curriculum):
  1. the job, with no Karen;
  2. the planner Karen (rungs C and E);
  3. the league ([§10.2](#102-the-league)).
- **Whole careers of six shifts,** so habits, moods and grudges matter.
- **A persona is drawn for every career.**
- **The careful corner** (caution ≥ 0.75 and pace ≤ 0.3, where the scripted cautious profile
  lives): the bots' own network trains on it, so those bots behave sensibly, but **no part of
  Karen ever meets one before the test.** The scripted cautious profile stays held out of
  everything, as today.

### 7.7 How "realistic" is measured: the scorecard

One tool (`tools/learn/scorecard.py`) reads shift records, the same way for bots and for people.

| Measure | Question | Target (fixed in [C1.4](#phase-1-measure-first), before any training) |
|---|---|---|
| Competence | does it finish shifts, at a human pace? | within your own range (Q4); no time-outs |
| Pattern | from a player's earlier shifts, can a simple predictor guess its next job, route and hiding spot? | clearly above chance |
| No repeats | same persona, same start: are two shifts ever the same? | never identical; route overlap under a ceiling |
| The band | how predictable is it? | between "chance" and "the scripted bots", moving toward people's number |
| Variety | can a classifier tell personas apart from one shift? | well above chance |
| Reactions | does it answer every trick's chore; does it dodge and back off? | every chore answered; rates near yours |
| Bot or human | can a classifier tell bot shifts from people's? | reported only, until about 10 players: lower is better |
| Glitches | falls through the floor, wedges, impossible actions | 0 |

## 8. Part 4: where she thinks you're going

PR #68, redesigned.

### 8.1 What PR #68 was

- **Her belief map** is a grid of 1.5 m cells with a chance in each: "you could be here".
- Ten times a second it does two things:
  - **correct:** take in what she saw, heard and found;
  - **predict:** spread the chances the way a person could have moved since.
- **The predict step is hand-written.** PR #68 learns it from recorded shifts: for every cell, "how
  likely are you to leave, and to which neighbour". It built five versions (M0–M4), trained
  "through the filter", so the number it's trained on is the number it's judged on.
- **About 70% built, then paused before training** (23 October): the code, the Python tools, 288
  recorded shifts, and its win rule written down in advance.

### 8.2 Why redesign it

Resuming it as it is would train the wrong model on a map that's gone.

| Since then | What changed | So |
|---|---|---|
| 0.2.0 | her map was cut to her part of the building; outside is one cell; she sees 36 m | the branch's predict step, its Python copy of the filter and all 288 recorded shifts fit a map that no longer exists |
| 0.2.0 | route guesses arrived: a handful of hand-weighted guesses at where you're heading, which Cut, Ambush and her run race to | two predictors now answer one question in two ways |
| 0.3.0 | her tricks leave chores at known places; cameras and Kenji see for her; fog blinds everyone | where you go next is mostly "which job or chore". A model that only looks at one cell and its neighbours can't see that |
| 0.3.0 | tricks are put down "on your way" (Spill, Block door, Camera, Ambush at a chore) | landing a trick now depends on knowing your way |
| 0.4.0 | personas, trained bots, real sessions, "everyone / you" | it was trained on three scripted bots; and it's the one model people's shifts can train directly |
| The results | Cut never fired in 144 shifts: chases ended in a catch first | prediction starts to matter once players get away, which better bots and Basic's catch target will bring |

### 8.3 The redesign: the destination first, then the walk

> **Destination model.** *What:* guess where someone is going, then how they'll get there.
> *In Kehai:* "you're probably off to reset the fog box, 30 m away, at a walk."
> *Elsewhere:* Google Maps offering "Home?" when you start driving.

**1. Destinations.** About a dozen at a time, all things she knows:
- each kind of unfinished job, the nearest one; the queue at the till;
- **her own chores** (the fog box, the stopped clock, the WC mop's rack, the far side of a
  blocked door, her camera);
- the breaker box when the lights are out; the coffee machine; the time clock; the ways out;
- your hiding spots (from your Ledger, or everyone's);
- "staying here", and "turned back".

**2. The choice.** The model gives each destination a chance, from: how far it is, what kind it
is, how long that job or chore has waited, whether you'd think she stands between you and it
([§8.8](#88-where-you-think-she-is)), the time left, which way you were heading when last seen,
and your Ledger.

**3. The walk.** Chance flows along the shortest way to each destination, at a learned mix of
speeds (sneaking 2.5, walking 4, sprinting 6.5 m/s), bending away from where you think she is.
Some stays put, for working and hiding.

**One model, four readers:**

```
                                  ┌─► the belief map's predict step      (where you could be now)
  destinations + their chances ───┼─► her route guesses, after any sighting (Hunt's run, Cut, Ambush)
                                  ├─► where to put a trick "on your way"  (Spill, Block door, Camera)
                                  └─► the brains' inputs: her three best guesses
```

**What's compared,** on the same evidence, side by side in the same shifts (PR #68's shadow filters):

| Id | Predict step | Question |
|---|---|---|
| last-seen | a blob round her last sighting | the floor |
| P0 | today's hand-written step | the shipped baseline |
| P1 | the hand-written step, its gains fitted to data, plus keeping away from her (PR #68's M1) | the fair baseline: same data, structure set by hand |
| P2 | destinations, learned from everyone | does knowing destinations beat spreading cell to cell? |
| P3 | P2, reading your Ledger | is the personal part personal? |

PR #68's M2–M4 (bigger models per cell, and a heading for every cell) are dropped. They asked
"does a bigger local model help?", and the destination replaces the heading.

### 8.4 Everyone, and you, again

The same split as the brain:

| | Everyone | You |
|---|---|---|
| What | the model's weights: how people choose where to go, and how fast | your Ledger: your routes, your hiding spots, how fast you answer each chore, how much you sprint |
| Example | "people reset the fog within a minute, unless she's in the way" | "you leave the fog for last, and you always go round by the tills" |
| Where | generated C# (`PredictionWeights.g.cs`), with a model card | the Ledger file |

### 8.5 How it's trained

> **Supervised learning.** *What:* learning from examples that come with the right answer.
> *In Kehai:* every moment she can't see you is an example, and the answer is where you really
> were. *Elsewhere:* a spam filter trained on mail people marked as spam.

**This model is supervised learning** (your 11 November idea, in those words): no trial and error,
only examples and answers, scored on players it never saw. The moments that matter most are the
ones just after she loses sight of you, so its report leads with them ([§11](#11-how-its-judged)).

- **Order.** Her senses are fitted first, on the hand-written step, and frozen
  ([§8.7](#87-how-much-to-trust-each-sense)). Then the mirror map's numbers
  ([§8.8](#88-where-you-think-she-is)). Then P1–P3. Each comparison's arms share everything it
  isn't testing.
- **Legs.** A leg is one walk from a stop to the next (a job done, a chore fixed, a hide, the
  clock). For bots the destination is known exactly: every choice is logged. For people,
  `tools/learn/legs.py` cuts their recorded track at interactions and long stops.
- **Stage 1: from legs** (minutes, no Unity). Fit the choice and the speed mix. This works on
  people's shift records as they are today, which is why this model learns from real sessions
  directly.
- **Stage 2: through the filter** (a few hours on the CPU). PR #68's method, kept: replay her
  whole belief in Python from a recorded trace, and adjust the same numbers to raise the chance she
  gives your true cell. Nothing is trained until the Python replay matches the C# filter (the
  parity gate), on the new map.
- **Data:** the three training profiles and Employee-1's personas, against the planner (rungs C,
  E). The cautious profile and the careful corner are held out.
- **Frozen before the Karens train.** The brains read its guesses, so it mustn't change under
  them. A new prediction model means a refresh round for the brains.

| Shadow filters: several belief maps fed the same evidence | kept |

| PR #68 itself | closed with a pointer here; the branch stays (Q5) |

### 8.7 How much to trust each sense

Added 11 November (D16).

> **Sensor model.** *What:* how far to trust a reading, and how wide to draw the circle round it.
> *In Kehai:* "a sprint heard through a wall puts you within 5 m, not 2."
> *Elsewhere:* your phone's blue dot, with a wider circle indoors than under open sky.

- **Today:** about 20 hand-set numbers decide how every observation lands on her belief map.

  | Sense | Today's spread (metres) | Where |
  |---|---|---|
  | Sight | 3 down to 0.8 as she grows surer | `Sensorium.cs` |
  | Hearing | 1 + 0.12 × the distance | `Sensorium.cs` |
  | Traces (a wet footprint, an opened door, a dropped item…) | 3 to 6, with a trust of 0.4 to 0.6 each | `Sensorium.cs` |
  | What a customer tells her | 2.5 + 0.25 × its age | `CustomerMemory.cs` |
  | "I looked and you weren't there" | a detection chance per cell | the sweep |

- **In 0.4.0:** the same formulas, with their numbers fitted to recorded shifts through the
  filter. It's supervised: the answer is where you really were. The trace and the replay it needs
  are built for the prediction model anyway.

  | Id | Senses | Question |
  |---|---|---|
  | S0 | today's hand-set numbers | the shipped baseline |
  | S1 | the same formulas, fitted | were the hand-set numbers right? |
  | S2 | S1, plus two inputs she already has: walls between, and lights off | does a noise through a wall deserve a wider circle? |

- **It adds no sense.** It changes only how far she trusts what she already senses. Training uses
  your true position, offline. The game never does.
- **Her percentages become checkable:** when she puts 70% of her belief in an area, you should be
  there 70% of the time (calibration, [§15](#15-terms)).
- **Fitted first, then frozen,** and held the same in every arm of the prediction ladder.
- **Ships as** `SenseWeights.g.cs` with a card, and in the model pack.

### 8.8 Where you think she is

Added 11 November (D17).

> **Opponent modelling.** *What:* keeping a model of what the other side knows.
> *In Kehai:* her guess at where you think she is. *Elsewhere:* a poker bot that tracks what its
> opponent believes it's holding.

- **Today she plans as if you always know where she is:** the hand-written step, and §8.3 as first
  written, bend your path away from her real position. People steer by where they last saw or
  heard her.
- **The mirror map** is a second, small belief map over the same cells, turned round: "where you
  think she is".
  - **Correct:** whenever she thinks you sensed her. You were in her sight, facing her way. She
    made a noise near where she believes you are (her run, a door, a trick's own sound). A phantom
    chime counts too: it's a false cue she chose to give you.
  - **Predict:** your picture of her spreads at her own speeds while you can't sense her.
  - **Only what she knows.** It's weighted by her belief about where you are, never by where you
    are. It sits under the source scan and the "moved player" test.
- **A few fitted numbers,** not a network: how likely you are to notice her, by sense and
  distance. Supervised: for bots the answer is what they really sensed (they already get
  `visible` and `heard`); for people, whether she was in view on your screen or in earshot.

  | Id | What she assumes you know | Question |
  |---|---|---|
  | K0 | where she is, always | today |
  | K1 | where she was when you last probably sensed her | is "last sensed" enough? |
  | K2 | the mirror map: that, spreading with time | does the spread help? |

- **Three readers:**

  ```
                               ┌─► the prediction model: you keep away from where you *think* she is
    where you think she is ────┼─► the brains' inputs: how stale your picture of her is, how wrong
                               └─► trick placement: Ambush and Lie in wait where you think she isn't
  ```

- *Example.* You saw her by the freezers 40 s ago and have heard nothing since. The mirror map has
  her somewhere round the freezers and the back aisles. She is in fact at the tills. Basic's
  inputs say "your picture of her is 40 s old and 25 m off": a moment to lie in wait on your way
  to the clock, not to chase.
- **Frozen with the prediction model,** which reads it.
- **Ships as** `MirrorWeights.g.cs` with a card, and in the model pack.

## 9. How the three connect

0.3.0 (PR #78) gave her new things to do and new things to know. The prediction model (was PR #68)
turns them into guesses. 0.4.0's two Karens act on those guesses.

```
  0.3.0: tricks, chores, places, new eyes
      │  each chore is a place she expects you          ▲  where to put the next trick
      ▼                                                 │
```

## 10. How training runs

### 10.1 One training run

```
N copies of the game (batch mode, silent)  ◄──►  the trainer (Python, tools/rl/.venv)
   • a career: one persona, six shifts, a fresh Ledger          • collects decisions and rewards
   • the learner asks the trainer at each decision              • improves the network (PPO)
   • everyone else is frozen and runs from generated C#         • saves a checkpoint every 50,000
```

The commands will look like this (planned; only `train.sh` exists today):

```bash
tools/rl/train.sh vital_r2_a 2 tools/rl/karen_vital.yaml --env-args -league tools/rl/leagues/vital_r2.json
python3 tools/learn/export_brain.py tools/rl/results/vital_r2_a --as KarenBrainVital
```

### 10.2 The league

One side learns per run; everyone else is frozen.

| Round | Who learns | Plays against | Decisions |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | nobody: demonstrations are recorded | scripted players against the planner | — |
| 1 | the players → Employee-1 | no Karen, then the planner (C, E) | 1.0 M |
| — | her senses, the mirror map, then the prediction model (fits, not league rounds) | recordings of the planner against scripted players and Employee-1 | — |
| 2 | Vital-1, and Basic-1 | scripted (3) + Employee-1's personas; Vital also meets the Survivor | 0.8 M each |
| 3 | the players → Employee-2 | the planner, Vital-1, Basic-1 | 0.4 M |
| 4 | Vital-2, and Basic-2 | everyone so far, old versions included | 0.4 M each |

- **Old versions stay in the pool** (half the careers against the latest, half against older
  ones), so nobody forgets how to beat an old opponent.
- **Stop early** if a round improves the validation metric by less than its standard error.
- **Basic's catch dial** is turned between rounds 2 and 4.

### 10.3 Seeds and who is held out

| Split | Seeds | Used for |
|---|---|---|
| Training | from 10000 | the training runs, the prediction model's recordings |
| Validation | from 20000 | choosing checkpoints and models, turning the catch dial, the scorecard's bands |
| **Test** (run once) | from 30000 | the results in [§11](#11-how-its-judged) |

**Never seen in training or tuning:** the scripted cautious profile, the careful corner's
personas, the test seeds, and (later) the held-out real players.

### 10.4 What it costs

**These are estimates** from one smoke run (about 13,000 decisions an hour for one copy, slowed by
errors). [C0.3](#phase-0-before-building) measures the real speed and corrects this table.

| Run | Decisions | Mac nights (2 copies, about 200,000 a night) | One 16-core PC (8 copies) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Employee, rounds 1 and 3 | 1.4 M | 7 | about 2 nights |
| The prediction model, her senses and the mirror map: recordings, training, their tests (the two new fits share the recordings and add minutes) | — | about 2 | the Mac does this |
| Vital, rounds 2 and 4 | 1.2 M | 6 | about 1.5 |

## 11. How it's judged

Written before any test run, and kept as written. Seeded, paired by seed × player × shift, with
standard errors. Negative results are reported as they come.

**Players in the test:** the four scripted profiles (cautious held out) and four groups of trained
personas (two from the training ranges, two from the careful corner).

### Her senses (fitted and tested first)

S0–S2 run beside each other on the same evidence, all on the hand-written step (P0).

**Primary:** the chance she gives your true cell over the whole shift (log p, every tick), on the
held-out players: the better of S1 and S2 beats S0. It wins if the 95% interval, resampled by
career, is above zero.

**Also reported:** calibration by area (she says 70%, you're there how often?); the score by
sense; milliseconds per belief tick. The winner is used in every arm below.

### What you know of her

K0–K2 run beside each other. The answer is what the player really sensed of her
([C1.6](#phase-1-measure-first)).

**Primary:** on the held-out players, how often the area she thinks you'd name for her is the
area where you last sensed her, sampled every 2 s while you can't sense her: K2 beats K1 by more
than 2 standard errors.

**Also reported:** the same against K0; P2 reading the mirror map against P2 reading her real
position (log p, out of view); in the game, catches from Ambush and Lie in wait, and how far she
was from where the mirror map put her at each catch.

### The prediction model (tested before the brains train)

In each test shift the planner acts on today's step (P0), and P0–P3 run beside it on the same
evidence, without the Director's hint. Every arm uses the same senses (the winner above) and the same mirror setting (the winner of K0–K2), so only the predict step differs. Today's
game (S0 with P0) runs beside them as a reference.

**Primary:**
1. **The chance she gives your true cell while she can't see you** (log p, out of view), on the
   held-out players: the better of P2 and P3 beats P1. It wins if the 95% interval, resampled by
   career, is above zero. This is PR #68's rule, kept.
2. **The personal part:** over shifts 3–6 of a career, P3 with your Ledger names your next
   destination more often than P3 with someone else's, by more than 2 standard errors.

**The report leads with the moment she loses sight of you:** is your real destination in her top
3 then, and what chance does she give your true cell 5, 10 and 20 s later?

**Also reported:** the right destination in her top 1 and top 3; the guess 1–30 s ahead; by time
since she lost you; inside fog; after a camera or Kenji sighting; milliseconds per belief tick
(her budget is 0.7 ms a frame).

**In the game** (planner on the chosen model against planner on P0, paired; no pass mark):
tricks that landed on your way, bets won; Cut fired and was right; catches and time to catch.

### Vital: the catch ladder (pacing off in every arm)

| Arm | What |
|---|---|
| The hunter | the hand-built planner with pacing off. The fair baseline: the same freedom, no training |
| Vital (both runs) | the trained brain |
| For reference | the planner and Basic with pacing on |

**Primary:**
1. **Time to catch** on the held-out players: Vital is faster than the hunter by more than 2
   standard errors, in both training runs. Shifts that end without a catch count as "not caught
   yet", not as missing ([Kaplan–Meier](#15-terms)).
2. **Fairness violations: 0.**
3. **The exploit check:** a fresh Survivor trained for 300,000 decisions against the frozen Vital.
   Reported: how often it survives, and how. A single trivial trick that beats her (standing
   somewhere she never looks) is a failure to fix.

### Basic: the fear ladder (pacing on)

| Arm | What |
|---|---|
| C | the planner |
| E | the planner with the Ledger: today's game |
| F | **Basic** (both runs) |
| F, Ledger wiped every shift | Basic without her memory of you |
| F, someone else's Ledger | Basic reading another player's Ledger |

**Primary:**
1. **Setpoint error** (how far fear sits from its target) on the held-out players: F beats E by
   more than 2 standard errors, in both runs.
2. **Adaptation:** over shifts 3–6 of a career, F with your Ledger beats F with it wiped, and
   beats F with someone else's. This is the proof that the personal part is personal.
3. **Catches a shift** inside your target (Q2).
4. **Fairness violations: 0. Stuck shifts: no more than E.**

**Also reported:** the trick mix (no trick above 40% of a shift's picks), panic, catches by cause
and place, seconds to first detection, combos found, how often she agrees with the planner,
whether the starting Ledger predicts held-out players' reactions better than the hand-set guesses
(PR #76's check), and everything per player group.

### The players

1. **The scorecard's targets hold** ([§7.7](#77-how-realistic-is-measured-the-scorecard)), on test seeds.
2. Karen's results against scripted and against trained players are shown side by side.

### The model pack

No training is needed for this one. With packs made from the build's own numbers:
1. a pack that's identical plays the same shift as the build (same seeds);
2. a tampered pack, an old inputs' version and an unknown trick name are each refused;
3. the gate refuses a candidate that's worse on a held-out metric, and passes one that's better.

### If it fails

- **Fitted senses don't beat today's:** the hand-set numbers stay, and everything after runs on them.
- **The mirror map doesn't beat "last sensed":** K1 is used. If neither helps the prediction
  model, its inputs are dropped and she plans as today.
- **P2 and P3 don't beat P1:** P1 ships if it beats P0, else today's step stays. The finding is
  "fitting the hand-written step is enough".
- **Basic doesn't beat E:** the game keeps E. The result is written up as it is.
- **Vital doesn't beat the hunter:** she stays out of the docs' claims; the players and the
  pipeline are still kept.
- **The bots miss the scorecard:** Karen's results are reported against the scripted players only.

## 13. Honest limits

- **The bots are still bots.** Until people play, "realistic" means "varied, patterned, and like
  you". Basic's score against them says little about how she feels to a person.
- **The Panic Index is a guess at fear.** Basic is paid in it. Until it's checked against real
  players, "she keeps you scared" means "she keeps you moving like a scared person".
- **It may not beat the planner.** Rungs D and E didn't. If Basic doesn't either, the game keeps
  E, and that's what gets written.
- **The prediction model may learn the bots' code** rather than how people move. A bot's
  destination is logged exactly; a person's is read off their track, so it's a guess.
- **A better guesser makes her deadlier,** and she's already too deadly for the bots. Basic's
  catch dial and PR #75's levers are what hold that.
- **Vital may find boring wins** (the clock camp). The guards are planned, not proven.
- **It's a lot of computer time.** About a month of Mac nights, unless Q1 finds something faster.
- **Friends are few.** Ten players refine the personas a little. The big gains need Steam's numbers.
- **The same seed doesn't give the same shift** after about two shifts in one session, so pairs
  share a start, not every step.
- **"Keeps improving" is only as fast as the data, and your go.** The fits are quick. The brains
  need nights of training and your word. With few players a new pack may be no better than the
  last, and the gate will say so.
- **The mirror map is a guess about a guess.** For bots its answer sheet is exact. For people,
  "she was on your screen" isn't "you noticed her".
- **Fitted senses fit the bots first.** They're refitted when people's shifts arrive.

## 15. Terms

| Term | What it is | How Kehai uses it | Real-world example |
|---|---|---|---|
| Reinforcement learning (RL) | learning by trying things and being paid for results | both Karens and the bots learn "what next" this way | AlphaGo learning Go by playing itself |
| PPO | a popular, steady RL recipe: improve a little each round, never jump far | the recipe ML-Agents runs for all three | OpenAI Five, the Dota 2 bots |
| Reward | the number the learner is paid | Vital: a catch. Basic: fear near its target | a chess engine: +1 for a win |
| Episode | one try, from start to end | one shift | one game of chess |
| Policy ("the brain") | the trained network that maps what she sees to a choice | `KarenBrainVital.g.cs`, `KarenBrainBasic.g.cs` | the part of a chess engine that picks the move |
| Action mask | greying out what isn't allowed | only legal (action, trick) pairs can be picked | a chess app that only lets you make legal moves |
| Warm start (behavioural cloning) | copy an existing player first, then improve | Karen copies the planner; the bots copy the scripted players | AlphaGo studied human games before playing itself |
| League | training against a pool of opponents, old ones included | bots and Karens take turns learning | AlphaStar's league for StarCraft II |
| Curriculum | easy lessons first | bots learn the job, then her | driving school: the car park before the motorway |
| Persona-conditioned | one network, told who to be | one bot network plays every persona | Forza's Drivatars, which drive like particular players |
| In-context adaptation | adapting by reading, not by retraining | Basic and the prediction model read your Ledger | your phone's keyboard: one model for everyone, plus your own dictionary |
| Blend by evidence (shrinkage) | mixing a group's average with one person's record, by how much there is of it | her guess for you, between everyone and you | a new restaurant's rating, pulled toward average until it has many reviews |
| Bayes filter | a map of chances, updated by what's sensed and by how things move | her belief map: correct, then predict | your phone's blue dot indoors, drifting between GPS fixes |
| Supervised learning | learning from examples that come with the right answer | the prediction model, her senses and the mirror map: the answer is where you really were, or what you really sensed | a spam filter trained on mail people marked as spam |
| Sensor model | how far to trust a reading, and how wide to draw the circle round it | the spread and trust of each thing she sees, hears or finds | your phone's blue dot, with a wider circle indoors |
| Calibration | stated chances match what happens | she puts 70% in an area, and you're there 70% of the time | a forecast of "70% rain" that's right 7 days in 10 |
| Opponent modelling | keeping a model of what the other side knows | the mirror map: where you think she is | a poker bot tracking what its opponent believes it holds |
| Model pack (over-the-air update) | a model's numbers sent as a data file, without a new version of the app | the general model, fetched when the game starts | an antivirus downloading new definitions |
| Publish gate | a test a new version must pass before anyone gets it | a pack must beat the current one on held-out players | an app update rolled out only after it passes its checks |
| Destination model | guess where someone is going, then how they'll get there | the redesigned predict step and route guesses | Google Maps offering "Home?" when you start driving |
| Shadow filter | a second copy fed the same inputs, to compare without changing anything | P0–P3 run beside her real belief | a new spam filter run silently beside the old one |
| Through the filter | training a part while the whole system runs, on the final score | stage 2: the prediction model, scored on her belief | tuning one instrument while the whole band plays |
| Held-out | kept away from training, only for the final test | the cautious profile, the careful corner, some real players | exam questions the students haven't seen |
| Picking chance (propensity) | how likely she was to pick what she picked | judging a new brain on old shifts, fairly | correcting a survey for who chose to answer |
| Kaplan–Meier | measuring "time until" when some never get there | time to catch, when some shifts end without one | Netflix measuring time until members cancel, when most haven't yet |
| Exploit check | training a fresh opponent just to break the finished model | a new Survivor against the frozen Vital | a red team hired to break in before attackers do |
| Model card | a short label: trained on what, how well it did | one beside each generated file | the nutrition label on a food packet |
| Checkpoint | a saved copy mid-training | chosen on validation seeds | a save point in a game |
